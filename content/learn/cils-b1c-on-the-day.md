The running order is decided before you walk in. CILS exams open with the listening tests, so the section that depends most on concentration is the one you meet while you are still settling. The written sections follow, and the spoken test is usually held in the afternoon. For B1 Cittadinanza the written part comes to about two hours fifteen in total, according to an Italian government information sheet.

## What you bring, and what you write with

Two rules from Siena's page on how the exams are run are worth memorising, because neither is negotiable in the room.

**An identity document.** You show it, and you sign the attendance sheet.

**A black pen.** Answers are written "solo con una penna nera" (only with a black pen). Answers in pencil or in another colour of ink are not valid. This is not a formality: on an answer sheet marked centrally in Siena, the colour of the ink is the difference between an answer that exists and one that does not.

## The sequence

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Identity check, attendance sheet signed |
| Ascolto | Two tests, about 30 minutes, each text played twice |
| Comprensione della lettura e Riflessione grammaticale | Two tests, 40 minutes |
| Produzione scritta | One email of {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}-{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} words, 40 minutes |
| Produzione orale | About 10 minutes, usually in the afternoon, recorded |

The spoken test is described by Siena as a conversation between the candidate and the somministratore (the person administering the exam) plus a monologue, and it is recorded. The recording travels to Siena; the marking is done centrally at the Centro CILS, not by the person sitting opposite you.

## If you need adjustments, the deadline is before the exam

This is the single most time-critical thing on this page. Siena's instructions to exam centres provide that candidates with disabilities may request the specific support they need to sit the exam, presenting the relevant medical documentation, and that the Centro CILS, together with the centre, undertakes to make available suitable means, specific logistical support, or extra time.

The documentation goes to the Centro CILS — the address published for it is gelo@unistrasi.it — and the timing is fixed: "durante il periodo delle iscrizioni alla sessione d'esame o al più tardi nei giorni immediatamente successivi alla chiusura delle iscrizioni" (during the registration period for the exam session, or at the latest in the days immediately following the close of registrations).

Two details that catch people out. The CILS Centre, not the candidate, informs the exam centre of the arrangement; and requests sent by candidates directly to the exam centre are not taken into consideration. If you need an adjustment, act while registration is open, not in the week before the exam.

## Who is allowed to sit it

Siena states that no particular educational qualifications are needed to take a CILS certification exam. What it does describe as necessary are well-founded general cognitive abilities, sufficient language skills in one's own language, and knowledge of the Latin alphabet. There is no age limit, and the exams are open to foreign nationals and to Italians resident abroad.

We should be honest about a gap here rather than paper over it: we found no official provision addressing candidates who are not literate in any alphabet. That is a genuine silence in the published material for an audience where it matters, not an implied exclusion.

## Where it happens

CILS exams take place at centres that have signed a convention with the Università per Stranieri di Siena. Abroad, the Italian Cultural Institutes and other referenti coordinate and organise them locally. Siena publishes directories of centres in Italy and abroad; registration is done with the centre where you intend to sit, not centrally.

## Questions readers ask

**How early should I arrive?**
No official arrival time is published; your exam centre sets the local arrangements and tells you the room and time. Ask them.

**Is the speaking test on the same day as the written sections?**
Siena describes the spoken tests as usually taking place in the afternoon. Confirm the day and time with your centre, since local organisation varies.

**What can I take into the room?**
The published rules cover the identity document and the black pen. Anything else - water, a watch, a dictionary - is not addressed in the material we verified, so ask your centre rather than assume.

**What if I am ill on the day?**
No official policy on absence, deferral or refund for this module was found on any page we could reach. Contact your exam centre as early as possible.
