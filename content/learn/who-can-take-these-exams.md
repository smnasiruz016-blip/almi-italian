Two different questions hide inside "can I take this exam". The first is whether an awarding body will let you enrol. The second is whether Italian law obliges you to produce a certificate at all. The answers come from different institutions and they do not track each other, so this page keeps them apart.

## Enrolling for CILS

Unistrasi imposes no educational entry requirement: *"Per sostenere gli esami di Certificazione CILS non è necessario avere titoli di studio particolari"* (*To sit CILS certification exams it is not necessary to hold particular qualifications*). There is no age limit, and the exams are open to foreign nationals and to Italian citizens resident abroad.

What the university does state as prerequisites is a short list about the candidate rather than their paperwork: well-founded general cognitive abilities, sufficient language ability in one's own language, and knowledge of the Latin alphabet.

That third item deserves saying out loud, because the citizenship audience includes people who read confidently in a non-Latin script and people who have had little formal schooling in any language. We found **no official provision for non-literate candidates** on any page we could reach. That is a gap in what is published, not a statement that such candidates are excluded — but if it describes you, raise it with the centre before you enrol rather than on the day.

## Enrolling for CELI

CVCL does not frame eligibility as a set of prerequisites; it frames it as three audiences, and you choose the family that describes you.

| Family | Audience as described by CVCL | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| CELI generici | *adulti scolarizzati* — schooled adults | A1 to C2 |
| CELI Adolescenti | Adolescents aged 12 to 18 | A2 to B2 |
| CELI Immigrati | Adult immigrants — *cittadini stranieri legati a un progetto migratorio stanziale*, foreign citizens tied to a settled migration project | A1 to B1 |

The adolescent series is the only one with a stated age band. For the other two we found no published rule barring anyone from choosing a family other than their apparent one; if that matters to your enrolment, ask your centre.

## Disability and special arrangements

The two bodies publish very different amounts of detail here, and the difference is worth knowing before you commit to one.

**CILS.** Unistrasi's manual for exam venues states that candidates with disabilities may request the specific support they need to take the exam, presenting the relevant medical documentation, and that the CILS Centre, working with the venue, undertakes to make available suitable means, specific logistical support, or extra time.

The procedure matters as much as the provision. Documentation goes to gelo@unistrasi.it during the enrolment period for the session, or at the latest in the days immediately following the close of enrolment. The CILS Centre then informs the exam venue — requests sent by candidates directly to the venue are not considered. Miss that window and you are relying on goodwill, so treat it as a hard deadline alongside the enrolment date itself.

**CELI.** The Regolamento has an article headed *Candidati con disabilità*, and its entire content is a cross-reference:

> "Si rimanda al documento Misure adottate per candidati con disabilità, parte integrante del presente Regolamento."
>
> *Reference is made to the document "Measures adopted for candidates with disabilities", an integral part of this Regulation.* — Regolamento degli esami CELI, art. 1.8

We could not locate that annexed document anywhere in public: it is not among the regulations CVCL lists, not among its downloadable forms, and not findable elsewhere on the university's domains. So we can tell you that provisions exist and are part of the regulation. We cannot tell you what they are — not extra time, not enlarged print, not a separate room — because no official source we reached describes any of them. Ask your centre and certificazione@unistrapg.it, and get the answer before you enrol.

## Who the law obliges to certify

Eligibility is one thing; obligation is another.

For **citizenship** under arts. 5 and 9 of L. 91/1992 — marriage or civil union, and naturalisation by residence — the applicant must attest Italian at not below B1 at the moment of filing. The Ministry of the Interior states that everyone who filed electronically from 4 December 2018 onward must show the requirement or show exemption. The Ministry's filing page lists as exempt: those who signed the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement); holders of the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* (EU long-term residence permit); and people affected by serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, illness or disability. Consular pages add a qualifier restricting the permit limb to permits issued by Italian authorities after 9 December 2010; that qualifier does not appear in the statute or on the Ministry's pages, and the conflict is unresolved.

For the **EU long-term residence permit**, the decree of 4 June 2010 applies to all foreign nationals requesting that permit and to the family members for whom it can be requested, and does not apply to children under fourteen or to a foreign national with serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, illness or disability, certified by a public health facility.

Do not carry the under-14 rule across. It belongs to the residence-permit decree. Whether minors are exempt from the **citizenship** B1 requirement is not something we could verify on any official source — check the current official page and ask your Prefettura.

## Frequently asked

**Do I need to live in Italy to sit these exams?**
No. Both bodies run networks abroad, and CILS is expressly open to foreign nationals and to Italians resident abroad.

**Is there a minimum age for the citizenship modules?**
Unistrasi states there is no age limit for CILS exams generally. CVCL defines its adolescent series by age but frames the immigrati series as being for adults. If a minor needs to certify, ask the centre first.

**I have a medical condition affecting language learning. Do I still need an exam?**
Possibly not — that exemption exists for both procedures, and for citizenship it follows the Constitutional Court's 2025 ruling. The Ministry's implementation requires certification from a public health facility, and excludes a certificate from a *medico di base* (general practitioner).

**Can I be refused enrolment for failing the CILS prerequisites?**
No official source describes a screening process. The prerequisites read as a description of who the exam is designed for rather than a gate — but the university publishes them, so raise any doubt with the centre.
