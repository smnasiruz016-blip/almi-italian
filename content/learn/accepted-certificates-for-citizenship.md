Two official Italian government lists of recognised certifying bodies are online at the same time, and they do not match. One has five names on it. One has four. Neither is marked as superseded. Before you pay an exam fee, you should know which list you are looking at and when it was published.

## The most recent list we found, dated 23 April 2025

The authority for citizenship purposes is the Ministry of the Interior. Its *Vademecum* dated **23 April 2025**, published by the Dipartimento per le Libertà Civili e l'Immigrazione, names five certifying universities and bodies, plus the CPIA network. The table below is our report of what that document names, not a transcription of it — the vademecum's section on *certificazione linguistica* came back to us reworded rather than quoted — so read the list on the source before you rely on it: <https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/sites/default/files/2025-06/vademecum_23_aprile_2025.pdf>.

| Certifying body | Certificate |
|---|---|
| Università per Stranieri di Siena | CILS |
| Università per Stranieri di Perugia | CELI |
| Università degli Studi Roma Tre | CERT.IT |
| Società Dante Alighieri | PLIDA |
| Università per stranieri "Dante Alighieri" di Reggio Calabria | Ce.Co.L (see note) |
| Centri provinciali per l'istruzione degli adulti (C.P.I.A.) | see caveat below |

Note on that cell: the certificate name **Ce.Co.L** is MAECI's, taken from the consular pages quoted in the next paragraph. Our reading of the vademecum records the Reggio Calabria body without a certificate name against it, and we do not attribute the name to that document.

The same five names appear on MAECI consular pages — the Italian embassy in Berlin and the consulate general in Munich both print them, including *"Ce.Co.L dell'Università per stranieri di Reggio Calabria"*.

Whatever the body, the level is the same: the vademecum repeats the statutory formula, *"non inferiore al livello B1 del Quadro comune europeo di riferimento per la conoscenza delle lingue – QCER"* — not below B1 of the CEFR.

## The older list: four bodies, still published

A Ministero dell'Interno / Prefettura document dated **June 2024** — still live — lists only four: Roma Tre (cert.it), Perugia (CELI), Siena (CILS), Società Dante Alighieri (PLIDA), plus the C.P.I.A. network. Reggio Calabria does not appear.

The MAECI's own central page on language certifications also describes four bodies, grouped in the association *Certificazione Lingua Italiana di Qualità* (CLIQ). It cites no decree and no date.

**We do not resolve this.** The dates are consistent with the five-body list being the current one for citizenship — a 2024 document giving four, an April 2025 vademecum giving five — but we could not find the ministerial act that added Reggio Calabria, or the date it took effect. If you plan to sit a Ce.Co.L exam for a citizenship application, confirm acceptance with your Prefettura or Consolato in writing first, and keep the reply.

Note also that some of our own older material lists four bodies. That was accurate against the 2024 document and is not accurate against the 2025 vademecum. This page is the current one.

## The school-qualification route

Art. 9.1 offers an alternative to a certificate: an Italian **titolo di studio** (educational qualification). The 2025 vademecum sets two conditions on it. The qualification must come from a public or state-recognised institution recognised by MIM, MUR or MAECI, *"a partire dalla scuola secondaria di primo grado"* — from lower secondary school upward. And:

> *"Il titolo di studio deve essere conseguito necessariamente in lingua italiana."*
>
> "The qualification must necessarily have been obtained in the Italian language."

A degree taught in English at an Italian university does not satisfy this on the face of that sentence.

## What we could not verify, stated plainly

Honesty about gaps is more useful here than a tidy list. Several official pages returned HTTP 403 to us and could not be read, and one document could not be reached at all.

- **The C.P.I.A. route.** Both the 2024 document and the 2025 vademecum list the CPIA network. What we could **not** transcribe in full is the vademecum's framing of it — whether a CPIA qualification is treated as a certificate from a certifying body or as an alternative *titolo di studio*. We do not state the mechanism. Ask your CPIA and your Prefettura.
- **The comparative table of certifications and levels.** The Ministry's June 2024 document points readers to a MAECI table at `linguaitaliana.esteri.it`. That host was unreachable for us. We do not describe its contents.
- **Which exam module counts.** No official government source we read enumerates acceptable exam modules. The law names a level and a set of bodies. Siena and Perugia each publish citizenship-branded B1 modules, and Perugia's official calendar prints one of them as "B1 CELI 2 i cittadinanza" — but no government text we found says a non-citizenship-branded B1 from a recognised body is excluded, and no awarding body says it either. Do not assume in either direction.
- **Whether the list changed after April 2025.** We checked the first page of the Ministry's circolari index and found nothing on the certifying-body list; we did not read the full archive.

## One consular sentence, and why we will not paraphrase it

MAECI consular pages carry this line after their list:

> *"Altre certificazioni provenienti dai suddetti Enti o da altre istituzioni non sono idonee"*
>
> "Other certifications from the said bodies or from other institutions are not valid."

The Italian is ambiguous. On one reading it excludes certifications from other institutions altogether; on another it also excludes other products of the listed bodies. We reproduce it and do not interpret it, because the two readings have different consequences for someone holding, say, a lower-level or non-standard certificate from a listed body.

## FAQ

**Which body should I choose?**
For the legal requirement, all bodies on the Ministry's list satisfy it identically. Nothing official ranks them, and nothing official says one is easier. Choose on sitting dates, on where you can physically sit, and on which exam format suits you.

**Does a B2 or C1 certificate work?**
The statutory wording is "not below B1", so a higher level from a recognised body is above the threshold. Confirm with your Prefettura, since acceptance is checked by a caseworker against their own list.

**Where do I check the list myself?**
The vademecum PDF and the consular pages linked above. Open the vademecum first — it is the most recent official list we found, and it carries its own date on the face of it.
