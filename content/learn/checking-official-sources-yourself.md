Check us. Not out of suspicion, but because the rules on these pages changed in 2018, again in 2025 and again in 2026, and because the office that decides your file reads the official page, not ours. This page is a map of where the official material lives, what each source can actually settle, and where the walls are — including the ones we ran into.

## Which source settles what

| Source | Authoritative for | Not authoritative for |
|---|---|---|
| **Gazzetta Ufficiale** (gazzettaufficiale.it) | The text of a law or decree as published, its number, its date and its entry into force | How an office applies it; consolidated text after amendment |
| **Normattiva** (normattiva.it) | The *testo coordinato* — a law as amended and currently in force | Nothing about procedure; and its index titles can carry errors |
| **Ministero dell'Interno — Dipartimento per le Libertà Civili e l'Immigrazione** (libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it) | The citizenship filing procedure, the attachment list, the exemptions, the accepted-certificate list, the circolari archive | The statutory text itself; local practice |
| The same department's **A2 test page** | The residence-permit language test: who must sit it, who need not, the booking route via testitaliano.interno.it | Anything about the citizenship B1 rule — a different instrument |
| **Prefettura pages** (prefettura.interno.gov.it) | How your specific Prefettura describes the procedure locally | The national rule, where the local page and the Ministry disagree |
| **MAECI / consular pages** (esteri.it domains) | What your specific Consolato will ask a person abroad to produce | The statute; and posts add qualifiers of their own |
| **Awarding-body sites** — Unistrasi (CILS), CVCL Perugia (CELI) | Exam format, sittings, fees, results, criteria | The law. An exam board cannot tell you what a Prefettura accepts |

The rule of thumb behind that table: **prefer the instrument over the summary.** A Gazzetta Ufficiale entry beats a consular news item about the same law. And prefer the office that will decide your file over any page at all, when the question is what will happen to your application.

## Two named starting points

For citizenship, the Ministry's own filing page is the single most useful page in the system: it names the portal (**cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it**, reached with SPID or CIE, or by registering if you live abroad), lists the mandatory attachments including the B1 certification, and lists the exempt categories. Its *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025 is the most recent list we found, dated 23 April 2025, of which certifying bodies count — the most recent, not necessarily the current one, since older Ministry pages still carry a shorter list and we could not find the instrument that changed it.

For the EU long-term residence permit, the same department's A2 page is the equivalent: it names the decree that governs the test (**DM 4 giugno 2010**), the booking site, and the categories not required to sit it.

The circolari index on the same domain is where operational changes surface first. That is where Circolare n. 19951 of 12 March 2026 — on the extension of the minors' declaration deadline — appears.

## How to read a disagreement between two official sources

You will find them. We did. Four examples, all live:

- **Four certifying bodies, or five?** The Ministry's April 2025 Vademecum and the MAECI consular pages list five. A Prefettura-hosted Ministry PDF from June 2024, MAECI's own central certifications page, and the Ministry's A2 page list four. We could not find the instrument that added the fifth.
- **A wrong law number on a Ministry document.** The June 2024 Prefettura PDF gives the 2018 conversion law as n. 136. MAECI and the Gazzetta Ufficiale record give **n. 132**. Cite 132.
- **A law's date.** The Gazzetta Ufficiale and the Ministry's circolare page say *Legge 27 febbraio 2026, n. 26*; several consular notices say 28 February, which appears to be the publication date.
- **An exemption qualifier that is not in the statute.** MAECI consular pages condition the EU long-term permit exemption on a permit issued by Italian authorities after 9 December 2010. That condition is absent from the statutory text and from the Interior Ministry pages.

The method, when you hit one: note the **date** on each page, note which body published it, prefer the instrument to the restatement — and then, if the disagreement affects your file, ask the deciding office in writing. Do not average two official sources. Do not assume the newer page supersedes the older one just because it is newer; sometimes the older one is simply still online.

## Be ready for the walls

Our own research ran into every one of these, and you will too:

- **interno.gov.it** (the main domain) and several **prefettura.interno.gov.it** pages returned HTTP 403.
- **Normattiva** would not serve any article beyond art. 1 of Legge 91/1992 — which is why we do not publish the residence-year thresholds in arts. 5 and 9.
- **Gazzetta Ufficiale article-level URLs** returned 403, which is why we cannot tell you which article of DM 4.6.2010 fixes A2 as the test threshold.
- MAECI's comparative table of certifications and levels sits on a host we could not reach at all.
- One Ministry circolare PDF (DAIT n. 59/2025, 17 June 2025) exists at a confirmed URL and returned 403 to us.
- Pages move. The citizenship portal address the Ministry publishes today supersedes the older "Portale ALI" endpoints that still dominate search results — and those old links still resolve.

**A 403, a dead link or a moved page is not evidence that a rule changed.** It is evidence that you need a different route to the same document: try the Gazzetta Ufficiale entry, the Ministry's circolari index, or the office itself.

## FAQ

**Which single page should I bookmark for citizenship?**
The Ministry's filing page on libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it, plus its circolari index.

**My consulate's page contradicts the Ministry's. Who wins?**
For what your consulate will accept from you, the consulate. For what the law says, the instrument. Where the two matter together, ask the consulate in writing.

**Can I rely on a language school's summary?**
For exam technique, sometimes. For a date, a fee, a deadline or an accepted-certificate list, no.
