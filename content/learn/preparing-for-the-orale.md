Your CILS oral is recorded. Unistrasi's exam-conduct page says so, and it also says the orals normally take place in the afternoon, after the written papers, and that the test consists of *"un colloquio fra il candidato e il somministratore e un monologo"* — a conversation between the candidate and the administrator, and a monologue.

That single sentence tells you more about how to prepare than most advice does. There is a person opposite you, there is a microphone, and there are two distinct things to do: talk *with* someone, and talk *by yourself*.

## What the CILS B1 Cittadinanza orale contains

The syllabus gives one test, lasting roughly ten minutes: a personal presentation of about one minute, and a dialogue of two to three minutes. Worth noting honestly — the two published component timings add up to less than the stated overall duration, and no official document we have seen explains the difference. Plan for the components as published and be unsurprised if the slot runs longer.

| Criterion (official, max 12) | Points |
|---|---|
| Efficacia comunicativa | 4 |
| Correttezza morfosintattica | 4 |
| Adeguatezza e ricchezza lessicale | 3 |
| Pronuncia e intonazione | 1 |

*Efficacia comunicativa* — whether the exchange actually worked — carries 4 points here, one more than it carries in the written task. That is the argument for keeping going. A candidate who stalls into silence to hunt for a perfect subjunctive is trading against the largest band in order to protect a share of another one.

### What is genuinely rehearsable

The one-minute presentation is the only part you can prepare almost entirely in advance. Build it, time it, cut it to length, and know it well enough to deliver it while nervous. Then stop rehearsing it, because a memorised recitation delivered at speed does the *pronuncia e intonazione* band no favours and can make the dialogue that follows sound like a different person.

The dialogue cannot be scripted, but it can be trained. Practise the machinery of conversation rather than the content: asking for repetition, buying a second to think, checking you have understood, correcting yourself mid-sentence and moving on. A self-correction that lands is not a lost point; an abandoned sentence usually is.

### The one point we cannot help you with

*Pronuncia e intonazione* is worth 1 of the 12 points, and our own speaking estimate cannot assess it — our tool reads a transcript of what you said, not the sound of it. We show it as not assessed rather than guessing at it. For that band you need ears: a teacher, a conversation partner, a language exchange, or at minimum your own recordings played back a day later. See our page on what AI feedback can and cannot tell you for the full statement of that limit.

## The CELI oral is a different room

At Perugia, the format is set by the CELI regulation: *"Il formato della produzione orale è un colloquio faccia a faccia fra un candidato e una commissione composta da due esaminatori"* — a face-to-face interview between one candidate and a commission of two examiners. So: two people, not one.

For CELI 2 (B1) the oral runs about ten to twelve minutes and has three components — an interview or conversation with the interlocutor, an image description task, and a role play set inside a clearly defined communicative situation. The CELI 2 i (B1) Cittadinanza module publishes a fuller oral, again at ten to twelve minutes, with five components: self-presentation, image description, intercultural mediation, civic topics, and dialogue. CVCL's exam description also indicates that candidates receive the oral materials shortly before the test rather than on the spot; our source for that detail is a paraphrase of the official PDF, so confirm it with your centre.

One CELI feature to expect and not misread: the regulation states that the commission assesses the performance on the spot *without informing the candidate of the score obtained*. Silence at the end of a CELI oral is the rule, not a verdict.

## Questions candidates actually ask

**Will they tell me how I did?**
At CELI, explicitly not. At CILS, results are published centrally: Unistrasi's candidate-facing results page says results are available after about three months and that no early assessments are carried out, while the centre-facing organisational manual describes a window running from 45 days — only for the B1 Cittadinanza module — up to four months, depending on level and candidate numbers. Those two official statements sit alongside each other unreconciled. Plan on the longer figure.

**Can I get extra time or other support?**
CILS provides for it: candidates with disabilities may request specific supports with supporting medical documentation, and the CILS Centre undertakes, with the exam venue, to provide suitable means, logistical support or extra time. There is a hard operational deadline — documentation goes to gelo@unistrasi.it during the enrolment period for your session, or at the latest in the days immediately after enrolment closes, and it is the CILS Centre, not you, that informs your exam venue. Requests sent by candidates directly to the venue are not considered. For CELI, the regulation has a dedicated article on candidates with disabilities, but it simply refers to a separate annexed document that we could not locate publicly; ask your centre or write to certificazione@unistrapg.it, and treat any specific list of accommodations you read elsewhere as unverified.

**Is being nervous going to cost me points?**
There is no criterion for composure. There are four criteria, and three of the four are about language. The fourth, *efficacia comunicativa*, rewards an exchange that works — which is a reason to ask the examiner to repeat a question rather than to guess at it.
