"How long does the whole thing take?" is the first question everybody asks about naturalisation by residence, and it is the one this page answers worst. What we can pin down precisely is the part that concerns language: the exact moment your certificate has to exist, what it has to be attached to, and how far ahead of that moment you need to have sat an exam. The rest, we will tell you honestly that we do not know.

## The one moment that is fixed by statute

Art. 9.1 of Legge 5 febbraio 1992, n. 91 — the article inserted in 2018 — requires applicants to produce their language evidence *"all'atto della presentazione dell'istanza"*, "at the moment of filing the application". The certificate is not something you send later when it arrives. It is a condition of the filing itself.

The requirement bites on applications filed from a specific date. A Ministero dell'Interno document published through the Prefettura network (dated June 2024) puts it plainly: everyone who filed the citizenship application electronically from **4 December 2018** onward must show the language requirement or show that they are exempt.

Art. 9.1 covers two routes, in its own words, *"ai sensi degli articoli 5 e 9"* — art. 5 is citizenship by marriage or civil union, art. 9 is naturalisation by residence. This page is about art. 9.

## The filing itself

The Ministero dell'Interno's current filing page instructs applicants to reach the portal with SPID or CIE, or to register on it if resident abroad, at **cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it**. That address supersedes the older "Portale ALI" endpoints under `portaleservizi.dlci.interno.it/AliCittadinanza`, which still dominate search results and third-party guides. Those legacy links still resolve. We could not establish whether they redirect, run in parallel, or are deprecated — so use the address the Ministry publishes today, and do not be confused when an older guide sends you elsewhere.

The same Ministry page lists the language certificate among the mandatory attachments to the electronic application, in these words:

> *"certificazione attestante la conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1 del QCER;"*
> "certification attesting knowledge of Italian, not below level B1 of the CEFR."

Alongside it the page requires an identity document; a birth certificate and a criminal-record certificate from the authorities of the country of origin; the receipt for the €250 payment; and the details of the electronic *marca da bollo* (revenue stamp) for applications filed in Italy. Once complete, the application is transmitted electronically to the **Prefettura** for people resident in Italy, or to the **Consolato** for people resident abroad.

On the €250: a Consolato Generale d'Italia notice of January 2019 records it as confirmed at €250 under art. 9-bis of Legge 91/1992, owed on all applications filed from 5 October 2018. That figure is from 2019; it is still repeated in the document list on the Ministry's current filing page, but treat any money figure as something to re-check on the day you pay.

## Working the timeline backwards from your filing date

Because the certificate must exist on filing day, the planning runs in reverse. The controlling variables are your exam board's sitting calendar and its results turnaround — neither of which is a legal deadline, and both of which the exam bodies describe as ranges rather than promises.

| Step | What it fixes | Where the timing comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Choose an accepted certificate | Whether the evidence will be accepted at all | Ministero dell'Interno Vademecum, 23 April 2025 |
| Enrol by the session's closing date | Which sitting you get | The awarding body's published calendar |
| Sit the exam | Nothing yet — a sitting is not a certificate | — |
| Wait for results and the certificate | The earliest possible filing date | The awarding body's published results window |
| File on the portal with the certificate attached | The statutory moment under art. 9.1 | Art. 9.1 L. 91/1992 |

Two practical consequences. First, a booked exam is not evidence; only the issued certificate is. Second, if you are close to a date that matters to you for another reason, build in the results window, and then build in more.

## What we will not tell you

**The residence periods.** We are not stating them. The residence-year thresholds for naturalisation live in arts. 5 and 9 of Legge 91/1992. Our research could not retrieve those articles: Normattiva served only art. 1 of the law on every attempt, and the Interior Ministry's main domain returned HTTP 403. We are not going to reproduce residence thresholds we could not read in the statute. Check the current official page and, if it matters to your file, the Prefettura that will decide it.

**The processing time.** There is one figure in our verified material and it comes from a January 2019 consular notice, describing a term that has been the subject of later legislative change and litigation. We are not publishing it, because publishing a 2019 number as though it were a 2026 number is exactly the failure this site exists to avoid.

**What happens to an application already pending when a rule changes.** No source we could read addresses transitional provisions. This is a real question for a real reader and we have no answer for it.

## Exemptions, in one paragraph

The Ministry's filing page lists three exempt categories: people who signed the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement) under art. 4-bis of D.Lgs 286/1998; holders of the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* (EU long-term residence permit) under art. 9 of the same act; and people with serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, illness or disability — the limb added after Corte costituzionale sentenza n. 25/2025. Several MAECI consular pages add a qualifier to the permit limb that does not appear in the statute. That disagreement matters enough to have its own page.

## FAQ

**Can I file and send the certificate afterwards?**
Art. 9.1 says the evidence is produced at the moment of filing. We have found no official source describing a way to file first and supply it later.

**Does the certificate have to be recent?**
No official source we could reach states any validity period for a language certificate for citizenship purposes. See our page on certificate validity for what the awarding bodies say and what remains unanswered.

**Is a specific "cittadinanza" exam module required by law?**
The law names a level — not below B1 — and a set of recognised certifying bodies. No official source we could reach requires a specifically branded citizenship module.
