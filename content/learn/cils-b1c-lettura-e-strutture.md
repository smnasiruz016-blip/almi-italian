Grammar does not get its own slot in this module. It is folded into the reading section, and the section's full official name says so: **Comprensione della lettura e Riflessione grammaticale** — reading comprehension and grammatical reflection. Candidates who prepare only comprehension, or only verb tables, are each preparing half of a single forty-minute block.

## The shape of the section

Siena's syllabus gives two tests, 40 minutes, and 350-500 words of text. The task types are information identification and a cloze — a text with gaps to complete.

The criteria document sets out how the twelve points are distributed:

| Test | Format | Items | Points per item | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lettura, prova 1 | True/false, as the criteria document labels it | 12 | 0.5 | 6 |
| Lettura, prova 2 | "Test a completamento con scelta multipla composto da 6 item" - a gap-fill with multiple-choice options, six items | 6 | 1 | 6 |
| **Section total** | | **18** | | **12** |

The second test is the grammar half. It is a cloze **with options given**, not a free gap-fill: you choose from the alternatives printed for each gap. That matters for how you practise. Recognising which of three forms fits a gap is a different skill from producing the form from nothing, and this section tests recognition.

## Forty minutes, unsupervised by a recording

Unlike listening, nothing external paces this section. The clock is the only constraint, and both tests sit inside the same forty minutes — there is no separate allowance for the cloze. Six items at one point each are, on paper, worth as much as twelve items at half a point, so a candidate who spends thirty minutes on the comprehension text and rushes the cloze can lose half the section in the last five minutes.

An ordering habit worth trying in practice, offered as our suggestion and not as anything Siena publishes: read the cloze text once early, so your mind has been in it, and then decide deliberately how to split the time rather than discovering the split by accident.

## What the texts are like

The syllabus fixes the volume — 350 to 500 words across the section — but we will not characterise the topics beyond that, because no official page we could reach describes the text types for this module in detail. The official sample paper on Siena's B1 Cittadinanza page is the reliable guide, and it comes with an updated answer key. If you want to know what the reading texts look like, that free PDF answers the question better than any description of it.

## How this section compares with the standard exam

An Italian government information sheet comparing the two B1 exams shows how much lighter this section is in the citizenship module: B1 Cittadinanza has 2 tests covering reading and structures together, where the standard CILS UNO-B1 has 3 reading tests plus 4 on analisi delle strutture (analysis of structures). Fewer tests is not the same as easier items; it means fewer opportunities to recover from a bad one.

## Questions readers ask

**Is the grammar section about verb tenses?**
Officially, it is a multiple-choice completion test of six items within a reading section. Siena does not publish a grammar checklist for the module beyond the syllabus itself, so work from the syllabus and the sample paper rather than from a list someone assembled elsewhere.

**Do I lose marks for a wrong choice in the cloze?**
No penalty scheme is published for this module, so we will not describe one. An unanswered item scores nothing, which is the one thing we can say with confidence.

**Can I use a dictionary?**
Nothing in the material we verified provides for one. Assume not, and confirm with your exam centre.

**If I fail only this section, can I retake just this section?**
No. This module does not allow capitalizzazione, so a failure in one skill means sitting the whole exam again in the next session.
