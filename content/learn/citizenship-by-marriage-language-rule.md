A belief that circulates widely and is wrong: that marrying an Italian citizen puts you on a gentler track, language included. It does not. The statutory language condition was written to cover the marriage route explicitly, by article number, in the same breath as naturalisation by residence.

## The words that put marriage inside the rule

Art. 9.1 of Legge 5 febbraio 1992, n. 91 — inserted by the 2018 security decree, D.L. 4 ottobre 2018, n. 113, converted with modifications by Legge 1 dicembre 2018, n. 132 — opens by naming the two routes it governs:

> *"La concessione della cittadinanza italiana ai sensi degli articoli 5 e 9 è subordinata al possesso, da parte dell'interessato, di un'adeguata conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1…"*
> "The grant of Italian citizenship under articles 5 and 9 is conditional on the person concerned possessing an adequate knowledge of the Italian language, not below level B1…"

**Art. 5 is citizenship by marriage or civil union. Art. 9 is naturalisation by residence.** The level is the same for both: not below B1 of the *Quadro comune europeo di riferimento* (Common European Framework of Reference, QCER/CEFR). There is no reduced level for spouses in the text we could verify.

One footnote on the legal citation, because you will see it done wrong on official paper. A Ministero dell'Interno document circulated through the Prefettura network in June 2024 gives the conversion law as *legge 1° dicembre 2018, n. 136*. MAECI's January 2019 consular notice and the Gazzetta Ufficiale record give **n. 132**. Cite 132.

## Where the marriage route's requirements actually get published

For applicants abroad, the operative page is usually the consulate's own "cittadinanza per matrimonio o unione civile" page — and those pages carry more procedural detail than the Ministry's central material. The Ambasciata d'Italia in Berlin and the Consolato Generale in Munich publish substantially the same content.

Those pages name the certifying bodies whose certificates they accept: PLIDA della Società Dante Alighieri; CertIt dell'Università Roma Tre; CILS dell'Università per stranieri di Siena; CELI dell'Università per stranieri di Perugia; Ce.Co.L dell'Università per stranieri di Reggio Calabria. That five-body list matches the Ministero dell'Interno *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025. Note that other official documents — including a June 2024 Prefettura PDF and MAECI's central certifications page — describe only four bodies, without Reggio Calabria. We treat that as a live disagreement, not a settled point, and we have not found the instrument that added the fifth body.

The Berlin page then adds a sentence that deserves care rather than paraphrase:

> *"Altre certificazioni provenienti dai suddetti Enti o da altre istituzioni non sono idonee"*
> "Other certifications from the said bodies or from other institutions are not valid."

The Italian is genuinely ambiguous. It appears to mean that certificates from those bodies *other than* the accepted citizenship-level certifications do not qualify — not that the listed bodies are unacceptable. We are reporting it as published and not resolving it. If your certificate is an unusual product from one of the listed universities rather than their standard citizenship-relevant certification, ask the post before you file.

## The exemption question that matters most to spouses

The Ministero dell'Interno's filing page lists three exempt categories: signatories of the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement) under art. 4-bis of D.Lgs 286/1998; holders of the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* (EU long-term residence permit) under art. 9 of the same act; and people with serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, illness or disability.

MAECI's marriage-route consular pages list four categories rather than three — adding holders of qualifications from recognised Italian educational institutions where teaching was in Italian — and, critically, they attach a condition to the permit limb: the EU long-term permit must have been **issued by Italian authorities after 9 December 2010**.

That condition does not appear in the statutory text of art. 9.1, nor on the Interior Ministry's filing page, nor on the Prefettura della Spezia page. Two arms of the Italian state are describing the same exemption differently, and the difference decides files.

| Question | Interior Ministry / statute | MAECI consular pages (Berlin, Munich) |
|---|---|---|
| Integration agreement signatories exempt? | Yes | Yes |
| EU long-term permit holders exempt? | Yes, unqualified | Yes, if issued by Italian authorities after 9 December 2010 |
| Serious language-learning limitations exempt? | Yes | Yes, with medical certification |
| Italian qualification listed as an exemption? | Not in the three-item list | Yes, as a fourth category |

**We are not resolving this.** If you hold an EU long-term residence permit that is older than 9 December 2010, or that was issued by another member state, put the question in writing to the office that will handle your application — your Prefettura if you are in Italy, your Consolato if you are abroad — before you decide not to sit an exam.

## Filing, and the timing rule

Whichever route, the evidence must exist at the moment of filing: art. 9.1 requires applicants *"all'atto della presentazione dell'istanza, ad attestare"* — to attest at the moment the application is lodged. The application itself goes through the portal the Ministry names today, **cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it**, reached with SPID or CIE, or by registering if you live abroad; from there it is transmitted to the Prefettura or the Consolato.

At a consulate, expect the original certificate to be shown and a *copia conforme* (certified copy) taken. The Berlin post publishes a €10.00 charge for that under tariff article 71. Consular fees and local requirements vary by post; do not carry that figure to another consulate.

## FAQ

**Do I need a lower level than B1 because I am married to an Italian citizen?**
No. Art. 9.1 names articles 5 and 9 together and sets one level for both.

**How many years of marriage or residence do I need?**
We are not stating them. Those thresholds live in arts. 5 and 9 of Legge 91/1992, and our research could not retrieve those articles from Normattiva. Check the current official page.

**Does the 2025 citizenship reform change the language rule for spouses?**
On the sources we could read — the consolidated text of D.L. 36/2025 and the consular notices implementing it — no language condition appears for the routes that reform governs. It is a negative finding on those documents, not a guarantee.

**Which exam should a spouse sit?**
The law specifies a level and a set of certifying bodies, not a branded exam. Compare the awarding bodies' own published formats before choosing.
