There are two exemption regimes in Italian law, and they are not the same regime. One relieves people of the **B1** requirement for a citizenship application under art. 9.1 of Law 91/1992. The other relieves people of the **A2** requirement for the EU long-term residence permit under the ministerial decree of 4 June 2010. They have different categories, different sources and different offices checking them. Carrying a category from one into the other is the single most damaging mistake we see, and it is one this page exists to prevent.

## Citizenship: exemption from B1

The Ministero dell'Interno's current citizenship-filing page lists three categories as exempt (*sono esentati*):

| Exempt category | Source of the category |
|---|---|
| Those who signed the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement) under art. 4-bis of Legislative Decree 286/1998 | art. 9.1 itself |
| Holders of the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* (EU long-term residence permit) under art. 9 of that decree | art. 9.1 itself |
| Persons affected by serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, illness or disability | Corte costituzionale, sentenza n. 25/2025 |

The first two are in the statute's own words. The Prefettura della Spezia states them the same way: excluded from the obligation are those who signed the integration agreement and holders of the EU long-term residence permit.

### The 2025 Constitutional Court ruling

The third category is new, and it is the largest change to the B1 rule since it was created. The Corte costituzionale struck down art. 9.1 in part, for failing to exempt applicants with serious language-learning impairments. The dispositivo opens:

> *"dichiara l'illegittimita' costituzionale dell'art. 9.1 della legge 5 febbraio 1992, n. 91"*
>
> "declares the constitutional illegitimacy of art. 9.1 of Law 5 February 1992 no. 91."

We stop the quotation there deliberately. The clause that follows — the words specifying exactly how far the ruling reaches — we could not obtain character-for-character, so we do not reproduce it. What we cite instead is the ruling's existence and the Ministry's implementation of it.

The dates, which matter if you are working out whether a decision on your file predates it:

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Deliberated | 30 January 2025 |
| Decided | 7 March 2025 |
| Published, Gazzetta Ufficiale 1ª Serie Speciale — Corte Costituzionale, n. 11 | 12 March 2025 |

### How the Ministry implemented it

The Ministry's *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025 describes the exempt group as those affected by serious limitations on language-learning capacity arising from age, illness or disability, attested by a certificate issued by a *struttura sanitaria pubblica* — a **public health facility**.

Our reading of the vademecum also records it as noting, per the Constitutional Court ruling, that certificates issued by a *medico di base* (general practitioner) are excluded — reported rather than transcribed, because that passage came back to us reworded. We are not turning it into an instruction: if the only evidence you hold is a letter from your GP, put that to your Prefettura before you file rather than assuming either way. Beyond "public health facility", the required format and the issuing body are not specified on anything we could read — ask your Prefettura what they will accept before you commission a report.

### The disagreement about the permit exemption

Here two arms of the Italian state say different things, and we are not going to pick a winner.

- The **statutory text** of art. 9.1, the **Ministero dell'Interno** filing page and the **Prefettura della Spezia** page all state the EU long-term permit exemption without any date or issuing-authority condition.
- **MAECI consular pages** (the embassy in Berlin, the consulate general in Munich) condition it on a permit **issued by Italian authorities after 9 December 2010**.

If you hold an EU long-term residence permit that is older than that, or one issued by another member state, this difference decides your application. Ask both your Prefettura and your Consolato, in writing, before filing.

The Berlin page also lists a fourth exempt limb the Interior Ministry pages we read do not: holders of qualifications from recognised Italian educational institutions where teaching was in Italian. Note that this overlaps with the *titolo di studio* route, which is a way of satisfying the requirement rather than being exempt from it — a distinction worth getting right on a form.

## Residence permit: exemption from A2

A different instrument, and a shorter list. Art. 1, comma 3 of the DM 4 June 2010 removes two groups from the decree entirely:

> *"Le disposizioni del presente decreto non si applicano: a) ai figli minori degli anni quattordici… b) allo straniero affetto da gravi limitazioni alla capacità di apprendimento linguistico derivanti dall'età, da patologie o da handicap, attestate mediante certificazione rilasciata dalla struttura sanitaria pubblica."*
>
> "The provisions of this decree do not apply: a) to children under fourteen years of age…; b) to a foreign national affected by serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, from illness or from disability, attested by a certificate issued by a public health facility."

The decree's own wording on limb (a) covers children under fourteen, including those born outside marriage, whether one's own or the spouse's.

Distinct from these are the categories the Ministry lists as **non tenuti allo svolgimento del test** — not required to sit the test, because they already hold acceptable evidence. Those are on our A2 pages, and they are not exemptions from the requirement.

## The thing we will not tell you

**Whether minors are exempt from the citizenship B1 requirement, and which minors.** The under-14 exemption we verified belongs to the A2 residence-permit decree. It is not in art. 9.1. Art. 9.1 mentions *età* (age) only as a possible cause of a language-learning limitation, which is a different idea altogether. Transplanting the under-14 rule into a citizenship application is exactly the error this page opened by warning about. We do not know the answer; ask your Prefettura.

## FAQ

**I signed an integration agreement years ago. Am I covered?**
The statute and the Ministry both state the exemption without a date condition. Keep your copy of the *accordo di integrazione* and any credit record, and confirm with the Prefettura handling your file.

**Does the disability exemption apply to the A2 test too?**
There is a parallel provision in the 2010 decree, quoted above, with its own wording. It is a separate provision under a separate instrument — same idea, different legal source. Do not cite the Constitutional Court ruling to a Questura handling a permit file.

**Can I claim an exemption instead of sitting an exam, to save time?**
Only if you fall within a listed category and can evidence it in the form the office accepts. An exemption asserted and refused costs more time than an exam sat and passed.
