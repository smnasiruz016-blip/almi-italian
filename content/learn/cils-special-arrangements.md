The deadline for asking is not the exam date. It is the registration window — and if you miss it, the arrangement you needed may simply not be in place on the day. That single sentence is the most useful thing on this page, and it is the part most often discovered too late.

## What can be requested

Siena's instructions to exam centres set out the provision in plain terms: "I candidati diversamente abili possono fare richiesta di supporti specifici necessari per svolgere l'esame, presentando la documentazione medica relativa alla disabilità" (candidates with disabilities may request the specific supports needed to sit the exam, submitting the relevant medical documentation). The same passage commits the Centro CILS, working with the exam centre, to make available suitable means, specific logistical supports, or extra time.

Three categories, then, in the university's own framing: equipment, logistical arrangements, and additional time. The document does not enumerate particular accommodations beyond that, so we will not publish a menu of examples as though Siena had approved one. Describe your requirement in your documentation and let the Centre respond to it.

## Who to send the request to, and when

This is where the procedure differs from most people's expectations. The request and the medical documentation go to the Centro CILS in Siena, at gelo@unistrasi.it — not to the local exam centre. Requests sent directly by candidates to the exam centre are not considered. It is the Centro CILS that then informs the centre where you will sit.

On timing, the registration page states the request should be made "durante il periodo delle iscrizioni alla sessione d'esame o al più tardi nei giorni immediatamente successivi alla chiusura delle iscrizioni" (during the registration period for the exam session, or at the latest in the days immediately following the close of registrations). That is a hard operational deadline, and it sits weeks or months before the sitting itself.

Registration deadlines for the 2026 sessions, as published:

| 2026 sitting | Register by |
|---|---|
| 19 February | 19 January |
| 14 April | 14 March |
| 16 June | 7 May |
| 22 July | 22 June |
| 21 October | 21 September |
| 3 December | 24 October |

For 2027 the published deadlines are 18 January, 8 March, 1 May, 22 June, 21 September and 23 October. Not every level is offered at every session, so confirm both the level and the deadline on the official calendar for your year before you plan around a date.

## A practical sequence

1. Choose the session and confirm your level is offered at that sitting.
2. Register through your exam centre — registration is always through a centre, in Italy or abroad, never centrally.
3. In the same window, email the Centro CILS at gelo@unistrasi.it with your medical documentation and a clear description of the support you need.
4. Keep the correspondence. If the arrangement is not in place on the day, the record of a timely request is what you will have to point to.
5. Ask the Centre to confirm what has been agreed, rather than assuming silence means yes.

The Centro CILS can also be reached at infocils@unistrasi.it and on the number the Centre prints as **tel +39 0577 240 100/11** for general enquiries.

## Who may sit a CILS exam at all

Siena sets no entry qualifications: "Per sostenere gli esami di Certificazione CILS non è necessario avere titoli di studio particolari" (no particular educational qualifications are needed to sit CILS certification exams). There is no age limit, and the exams are open to foreign nationals and to Italians resident abroad.

The prerequisites the university does state are general: well-founded general cognitive abilities, sufficient language skills in one's own language, and knowledge of the Latin alphabet. There is a real gap here that we would rather name than paper over: we found no official provision for candidates who are not literate, in any alphabet. That absence is a gap in the published material, not a statement that such candidates are excluded, and it matters for exactly the population most likely to need a B1 certificate for a citizenship file. If it affects you, ask the Centro CILS directly and get the answer in writing.

## What happens on the day

The standing exam-day rules apply to everyone. Sittings begin with the listening tests. Answers must be written in black pen only — pencil or coloured ink invalidates them. You need identification and must sign the attendance sheet. Oral tests are usually held in the afternoon and are recorded, and consist of a conversation between the candidate and the administrator plus a monologue. If any of these mechanics is the thing you need adjusted, that is precisely what the documented request is for.

## Questions people actually ask

**Can my exam centre grant extra time on the day?**
Nothing on the official pages we read gives a centre that power on its own. The documented route runs through the Centro CILS, which then informs the centre.

**What if my documentation is not in Italian?**
Not addressed by any official source we could read. Ask the Centro CILS when you write, and allow time for the answer inside the registration window.

**Does a disability exempt me from the language requirement for citizenship?**
That is a different question with a different decision-maker. Exemption from the citizenship language requirement is a matter for the Ministry of the Interior and the prefettura, not for an exam board, and it is covered on our exemptions page. An exam accommodation and a legal exemption are not the same thing.

**I missed the registration window. Can I still ask?**
The published wording allows the days immediately after registrations close, and no more than that. If you are past it, contact the Centre anyway and be ready to plan for the following session.
