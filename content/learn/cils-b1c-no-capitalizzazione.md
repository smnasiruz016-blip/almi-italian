"Per questo livello non è consentita la 'capitalizzazione'. Se l'esame non è superato in qualche abilità, questa non si potrà recuperare, ma si dovrà sostenere di nuovo tutto l'esame nella sessione successiva."

That is Siena, on its own page for the B1 Cittadinanza module. In English: for this level, capitalizzazione (banking a passed skill) is not permitted; if the exam is not passed in some skill, that skill cannot be recovered — the whole exam must be taken again in the next session.

If you read one sentence about this exam, read that one. It is the most consequential published fact for a citizenship candidate, and it is the fact most often lost when the module gets described loosely as "the B1 exam".

## What capitalizzazione is, and where it does exist

Capitalizzazione is the mechanism by which a candidate who passes some skills and fails others keeps the passes and returns only for the failures. It is a real feature of CILS — just not of this module.

An Italian government information sheet sets the two exams side by side. B1 Cittadinanza is described as being without capitalizzazione, while for B1 Standard it says: "Con capitalizzazione – se l'esame non è superato in qualche abilità, si potrà sostenere di nuovo l'esame solo nella parte non sufficiente" (with capitalizzazione: if the exam is not passed in some skill, the exam may be retaken only in the part that was insufficient).

For the standard exams where banking does apply, Siena's published retention window is itself inconsistent across two of its own pages — the assessment page describes 18 months, while the Linee guida describe one year from the date of the first exam sat. Neither page is reliably dated and we are not resolving the difference. It does not affect B1 Cittadinanza, which has no banking at all, but it matters if you are choosing between the two exams.

## What it changes about preparing

Four skills, one sitting, no partial credit carried forward. Three practical consequences follow.

**No sacrificial section.** A candidate who is strong in listening and reading and hopes to scrape through the writing is not gambling on a mark; they are gambling on the whole certificate. Balanced preparation is not a virtue here, it is arithmetic.

**A retake is a full retake.** Not the failed skill — everything, in the next session. Whether the 100 euro fee is payable again is not something we could verify on any official page; ask your exam centre before you assume either way.

**Your worst day is the one that counts.** There is no aggregating across sittings.

## What it changes about your timetable

There are six CILS sessions a year and B1 C is offered in all six, which limits the damage: in 2026 the sittings run 19 February, 14 April, 16 June, 22 July, 21 October and 3 December, and 2027 follows a similar pattern. But each session has its own registration deadline roughly a month before, and results are not immediate.

If your citizenship application has a date attached to it — a filing you intend to make, a permit stage, a family plan — build the timetable around the possibility of a full retake, not around passing first time. A candidate who leaves exactly one sitting between now and their filing has no margin at all, because the language certificate is required at the moment the application is filed.

## Questions readers ask

**Is there really no way to keep a passed skill?**
Not at this level, according to Siena's own page for the module. The standard CILS UNO-B1 allows it; this module does not.

**How soon can I retake?**
Siena's wording is "nella sessione successiva" (in the next session). How many attempts are allowed, and whether any waiting period applies beyond that, is not published on any page we could reach.

**Would the standard B1 exam be safer, then?**
It is a different trade-off, not a safer one: the standard exam is considerably longer and has more tests, but does allow banking. That comparison has its own page.

**Do I pay the full fee again?**
Unverified. The published fee for B1 Cittadinanza is 100 euro in Italy and abroad; no official text we found says what a resit costs.
