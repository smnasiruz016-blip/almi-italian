One sentence added to a 1992 statute in December 2018 is the reason this website exists. Everything else — which exam, which centre, which score — sits downstream of it. This page sets out that sentence, where it came from, and exactly which applications it touches.

## Where the requirement comes from

The language condition is **art. 9.1 of Legge 5 febbraio 1992, n. 91** (Law 91/1992, the Italian citizenship act). It was not in the original law. It was inserted by art. 14, comma 1, lett. a-bis) of **D.L. 4 ottobre 2018, n. 113** — the *decreto sicurezza* (security decree) — which was converted with modifications by a law of 1 December 2018.

That conversion law is **n. 132**, per the Gazzetta Ufficiale record and per a MAECI consular notice of January 2019. Note a live discrepancy: a Ministero dell'Interno / Prefettura document still online, dated June 2024, gives the conversion law as *"legge 1° dicembre 2018, n. 136"*. We cite 132 and flag the difference rather than resolve it; if a caseworker quotes 136 at you, this is why.

The operative words of art. 9.1, as reproduced by an Italian consulate:

> *"La concessione della cittadinanza italiana ai sensi degli articoli 5 e 9 è subordinata al possesso, da parte dell'interessato, di un'adeguata conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1 del Quadro Comune Europeo di Riferimento per la conoscenza delle lingue (QCER)."*
>
> "The grant of Italian citizenship under articles 5 and 9 is conditional on the person concerned possessing an adequate knowledge of the Italian language, not below level B1 of the CEFR."

## Which applications it touches

The statute names two articles, and only two.

| Route | Statutory basis | Language condition |
|---|---|---|
| Naturalisation by residence | art. 9, L. 91/1992 | Yes — B1 or above |
| Citizenship by marriage or civil union | art. 5, L. 91/1992 | Yes — B1 or above |
| Other routes | not named in art. 9.1 | Not by the terms of this article |

The phrase in the law is *"ai sensi degli articoli 5 e 9"* (under articles 5 and 9). Article 5 is the marriage and civil-union route; article 9 is naturalisation by residence. The Prefettura della Spezia restates the same rule in the same terms. Art. 9.1 does not by its own words extend to other routes — but that is a reading of what the article says, not a statement that no other Italian provision could ever impose a language condition elsewhere. If you are applying by a route other than articles 5 and 9, confirm with your Prefettura or Consolato.

### The date that decides whether it applies to you

The Ministero dell'Interno document of June 2024 puts the cut-off plainly:

> *"In sostanza, tutti coloro che hanno presentato telematicamente la domanda di cittadinanza a partire dal 4 dicembre 2018, devono dimostrare di possedere il suddetto requisito di conoscenza linguistica o di esserne esenti."*
>
> "In substance, all those who filed the citizenship application electronically from 4 December 2018 onward must demonstrate possession of the said language requirement or that they are exempt from it."

4 December 2018 is the date the conversion law entered into force. The decree itself was in force from 5 October 2018.

## What counts as proof

Art. 9.1 gives two alternatives. Either a **titolo di studio** (educational qualification) issued by a public or state-recognised Italian educational institution recognised by the education and foreign ministries, or a **certificazione** (certificate) issued by an *ente certificatore riconosciuto* (recognised certifying body). Which bodies those are is a separate question with a live disagreement between official lists — see our page on accepted certificates.

Two things the law does **not** say, both of which are widely misreported:

- **It does not name an exam.** The statute fixes a level (not below B1) and a category of issuer. Neither the statute nor the Ministry material we could verify says that only a module branded "cittadinanza" counts. Perugia and Siena both publish citizenship-specific B1 modules, and those are exam-provider products, not legal categories.
- **It does not set the residence periods.** The famous year-counts for naturalisation and for marriage sit in articles 5 and 9 themselves, which we could not retrieve from an official consolidated text. We do not state them anywhere on this site until we can.

### Timing: at the moment of filing

Art. 9.1 requires applicants *"all'atto della presentazione dell'istanza, ad attestare…"* — at the moment of filing the application, to attest. This is not a document you supply later on request. Plan the exam date backwards from the filing date, allowing for results and certificate turnaround.

## Who is relieved of the obligation

Art. 9.1 attaches the duty to applicants who have **not** signed the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement, art. 4-bis of Legislative Decree 286/1998) and who are **not** holders of the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* (EU long-term residence permit, art. 9 of the same decree). Since 2025 a third category exists, following a Constitutional Court ruling on applicants with serious language-learning impairments. Because the details carry conditions and one unresolved contradiction between ministries, they have their own page.

## Questions readers actually ask

**Is B1 the same requirement for the residence permit?**
No. The EU long-term residence permit runs on a different instrument and a different level — A2 — under art. 9 of Legislative Decree 286/1998 and the ministerial decree of 4 June 2010. Do not read across between the two regimes.

**Does my B1 certificate expire for citizenship purposes?**
No official source we could reach addresses the validity period of a language certificate for citizenship. Siena's own FAQ says a CILS certificate *"attualmente"* (currently) has no expiry, and Perugia states CELI certificates have no expiry — but those are the awarding bodies speaking about their own certificates, not the Ministry speaking about the application. Ask your Prefettura before assuming an old certificate will be accepted.

**Was the language rule changed by the 2025 citizenship reform?**
Nothing in the sources we checked for the 2025 reform imposes or alters a language condition. That is a negative finding on the documents we could open, not a guarantee; check the current official page before filing.

**Where is the authoritative text?**
The Ministero dell'Interno's own pages, and the Gazzetta Ufficiale record for Legge 132/2018. Every URL we relied on is listed above. Open them yourself before you file.
