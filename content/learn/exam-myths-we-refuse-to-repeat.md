Two official documents describe the same CILS exam and give it two different totals. Siena's own criteria document for B1 Cittadinanza adds up to {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} points. An Italian government information sheet says both B1 exams are marked out of 100, with 55 to pass and a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} per skill. Neither of those is a myth. They are both published, they disagree, and no official document reconciles them.

Most exam myths are not lies invented by bad people; they are confident sentences filling a gap the awarding bodies left open. Below are the ones we have decided never to repeat, each with what the record actually says. Every one is a myth the official sources themselves contradict or leave open.

## 1. "You need X out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} to pass CILS B1 Cittadinanza"

Siena publishes no pass mark for the Cittadinanza module, and no per-skill minimum. Our own product shows a {{CILS_B1C_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} floor per skill and a {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} total, and those numbers are **ours**: we derived them from the official standard CILS UNO–B1 rule of {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points, 55 to pass and a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} per skill — 11 of 20 is 55 per cent, 55 per cent of 12 is 6.6, rounded up to 7, and four skills at 7 is 28. Anyone quoting you an official figure for this module is quoting a school or a video, not the university.

## 2. "You can bank the skills you passed and resit only the rest"

Not at this level, and this is the most consequential fact on the page for a citizenship candidate. Unistrasi: *"Per questo livello non è consentita la 'capitalizzazione'"* — for this level, banking is not allowed. If one skill is not passed, the entire exam is taken again at the next session. The standard CILS UNO–B1 does allow *capitalizzazione*; the Cittadinanza module does not.

CELI's regulation works differently again: it provides that a candidate who passes one of the two parts — written or oral — and fails the other may capitalise the passed part for one year, resitting only the failed part at 70 per cent of the full fee, at the same level and same exam type. No official source we have found states explicitly whether that mechanism operates for the CELI cittadinanza module, so do not assume it does.

## 3. "CELI Impatto is A2 and CELI 1 is A1"

Reversed, and reversed on many third-party sites. CVCL's own page prints CELI Impatto at A1, CELI 1 at A2, CELI 2 at B1, CELI 3 at B2, CELI 4 at C1 and CELI 5 at C2. Booking the B1 exam for a citizenship application means the one the official calendar prints as "B1 CELI 2 i cittadinanza".

## 4. "Every exam centre sets its own price"

Officially unsupported, and differently unsupported for each exam. Unistrasi states a flat fee of €100 for CILS B1 Cittadinanza, in Italy and abroad, and its manual for exam venues tells centres to collect the fee set in the convention, not one of their own. CVCL publishes no euro figure for CELI at all — the amount is set by CVCL's conventions with each venue, is owed entirely to CVCL, and centres are required to state the exam fee separately from any add-ons such as preparation courses or administrative costs. Ask your centre for the number and ask for it itemised.

## 5. "CILS B1 Cittadinanza results come back in 45 days"

The 45-day figure is real, but it is the fast end of a range in a document written for exam centres, describing a window from 45 days — only for the B1 Cittadinanza module — up to four months. The page written for candidates says results appear after about three months, with no early assessments. Plan around the longer figure. For CELI, the regulation says roughly 90 working days, counted from CVCL's receipt of the scripts rather than from exam day.

## 6. "You can appeal a bad mark"

No official re-mark or appeal procedure is published by either body. That is not the same as saying none exists, and it is not the same as having no rights. Unistrasi publishes a right to view your papers within 30 days of results, by writing to trasparenzacertificazioni@unistrasi.it with your ID, personal details and matricola number and paying the access fee under Law 241/90. CELI's regulation provides for requests for clarification to certificazione@unistrapg.it and for statutory access to records. Viewing your paper is not re-marking it.

## 7. "The law requires an exam with 'cittadinanza' in its name"

The law requires a *level* and a *recognised issuing body* — knowledge of Italian not below B1 of the CEFR, evidenced by a qualification or a certification from a recognised *ente certificatore*. It does not, in the text we have seen, name a branded citizenship module.

That is not permission to book whatever you like. The number of recognised bodies is itself contested in live official documents: the Interior Ministry's *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025 lists five certifying bodies plus the CPIA network, while a Ministry document from June 2024 still online lists four. Before you pay for anything, check the current official page, and ask your Prefettura or consulate if there is any doubt about your module.

## 8. "One of the two exams is easier"

No official source says this, in either direction, about either exam. CVCL publishes nothing comparing CELI with CILS, the CILS venue manual mentions CELI nowhere, and the Interior Ministry treats certificates from the recognised bodies as routes to the same B1 requirement without ranking them. The absence is the finding. Choose on sittings, location, format and cost.

## Three more, briefly

**Does my certificate expire?**
The two bodies say no, and one of them says it carefully. Unistrasi's FAQ: *"Attualmente non ha una scadenza"* — currently it has no expiry. Keep the word "currently"; the university itself does not commit to permanence. CVCL states that CELI certificates have no expiry. Whether a certificate has any validity limit for the purposes of a citizenship application is a separate question, and no official source we could reach addresses it — so we will not answer it.

**Isn't the shorter citizenship exam the soft option?**
It is shorter — roughly two hours fifteen for CILS B1 Cittadinanza against about three hours fifty-five for the standard B1 — and it is all-or-nothing, with no banking. Shorter and riskier is a trade-off, not a discount.

**If a site gives me a number nobody official publishes, is it wrong?**
Not necessarily. But you should be able to find out who published it. That test alone removes most of what circulates.
