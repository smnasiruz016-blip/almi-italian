Far less is publicly documented about CILS DUE-B2 than about the B1 level below it, and a page that hid that fact would be doing you a disservice. Below is what the Università per Stranieri di Siena states officially about its B2 certification, followed by an equally explicit list of what we could not confirm and where to look for it yourself.

## The level and its official name

Siena's certification is called CILS DUE-B2. The university uses its own numbering — A1, A2, CILS UNO-B1, CILS DUE-B2, CILS TRE-C1, CILS QUATTRO-C2 — and groups those six into three bands. DUE-B2 belongs to the "autonomous" band, together with UNO-B1. The certification as a whole is described by Siena as "un titolo di studio rilasciato dall'Università per Stranieri di Siena che attesta il grado di competenza linguistico-comunicativa in italiano come L2" (a qualification issued by the University for Foreigners of Siena attesting the degree of communicative competence in Italian as a second language).

Every CILS level stands on its own. You do not have to hold UNO-B1 before sitting DUE-B2, and you are not required to have studied at any particular institution. Siena states that no specific educational qualifications are needed to sit a CILS exam and sets no age limit; the prerequisites it does name are well-founded general cognitive skills, sufficient literacy in your own language, and knowledge of the Latin alphabet.

## Sittings

Siena runs six sessions a year and publishes a calendar for each year naming which levels are offered when. For 2026, B2 appears in the April session — where the calendar distinguishes B2 standard from a separate B2 adolescenti (adolescents) module — and in the two sessions listed as covering all levels, 16 June and 3 December.

| 2026 session | Registration deadline as published |
|---|---|
| 14 April | 14 March |
| 16 June | 7 May |
| 3 December | 24 October |

The 2027 calendar names 8 April, 10 June and 2 December among its six dates, with deadlines of 8 March, 1 May and 23 October. Deadlines mostly fall about a month before the sitting, but the June and December ones fall considerably earlier, which is exactly the trap that catches people planning around a summer sitting. Confirm the level, the date and the deadline on the official calendar for your year before you make any plan that depends on them.

## What we cannot tell you about B2

Three things a candidate reasonably wants, and which we will not invent:

- **The pass mark.** The scoring rule we can verify — {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points, 55 to pass, a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} in each skill — is published in Siena's Linee guida for the standard CILS UNO-B1. We did not verify that the same figures govern DUE-B2. Read the Linee guida PDF for the level you are sitting rather than assuming the B1 rule transfers.
- **The exam's structure and duration.** We hold a verified component-by-component breakdown for B1 only. For B2, check Siena's own level page and the sample materials it publishes.
- **The fee.** Siena publicly quotes a figure only for the B1 citizenship module. For every other level, including B2, no current official price was available to us. Your exam centre collects the fee set in its convention with the university, so ask the centre where you intend to sit.

Anywhere you see a confident B2 pass mark or price quoted by a school, a forum or a video, treat it as unsourced until you find it on a Unistrasi page or PDF.

## Registration and exam day

Registration is always through an exam centre, in Italy or abroad — there is no central candidate portal. Siena's exam-day rules apply across levels: sittings begin with the listening tests, answers must be written in black pen only (pencil or coloured ink invalidates them), you need identification and must sign the attendance sheet, and oral tests usually take place in the afternoon and are recorded. The oral consists of a conversation between candidate and administrator plus a monologue.

Marking is central. All papers are assessed at the Centro CILS in Siena, which wrote the assessment criteria — your centre neither marks nor influences your result.

## After the exam

Siena's candidate-facing results page says results are available after about three months and that no early assessments are carried out. Results are looked up online with your matricola (candidate registration number) and date of birth, and the certificate is collected from the centre where you sat. If a passed skill needs re-sitting, DUE-B2 is one of the standard exams where capitalizzazione applies — though how long banked skills remain valid is disputed between two official pages, which we lay out separately rather than resolve.

On validity, Siena's FAQ answers the question directly: the certificate "attualmente non ha una scadenza" (currently has no expiry). Keep the word "currently" — the university itself does not commit to permanence.

## Questions people actually ask

**Do I need B2 for Italian citizenship?**
The level required for citizenship is B1. Siena's own pages describe B1 as the level required for obtaining Italian citizenship. A higher certificate is not a substitute for checking what the Ministry's current list accepts — that is a legal question, handled in our citizenship section.

**Is B2 harder than B1?**
They certify different levels of the Common European Framework, and we will not rank exams by difficulty. What matters practically is that each level is autonomous and that the exams differ in length and in the number of tasks per skill.

**Can I sit B2 abroad?**
CILS exams take place at centres that have signed a convention with Siena; abroad, Italian Cultural Institutes and other partners organise the sittings locally. The level available at a given centre in a given session is a question for that centre.
