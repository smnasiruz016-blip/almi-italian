Two questions get compressed into one worry here, and they have different answers from different people.

**Question one: does the certificate itself expire?** That is a question for the university that issued it, and both of the awarding bodies we cover answer it on their own websites.

**Question two: will a Prefettura or Consolato accept a certificate issued years ago as evidence for a citizenship application?** That is a question for the Italian state, and no official source we could reach addresses it at all.

Keep them apart. The first has an answer; the second does not.

## What the awarding bodies say about their own certificates

**Siena (CILS).** The Università per Stranieri di Siena FAQ answers it directly:

> *"Qual è la validità del certificato? Ha una scadenza? Attualmente non ha una scadenza."*
> "What is the certificate's validity? Does it have an expiry? Currently it does not have an expiry."

The word *attualmente* — "currently" — is the university's own, and it is worth preserving. Siena is describing its present position, not making a permanent commitment.

**Perugia (CELI).** CVCL states on its exams page that *"i Certificati CELI non hanno scadenza"* — "CELI certificates have no expiry". An Italian government page, the Istituto Italiano di Cultura in Amsterdam, describes the CELI in the same terms: recognised by the Italian Government, internationally accepted, and with no expiry.

One thing worth flagging about the CELI statement: the *Regolamento degli esami CELI*, the regulation itself, contains **no article on validity or expiry**. The "non hanno scadenza" sentence lives on the CVCL website, not in the regulation. That does not make it wrong. It does mean the statement is web-page policy rather than regulation text.

| | Siena — CILS | Perugia — CELI |
|---|---|---|
| Published position on expiry | "Attualmente non ha una scadenza" | "i Certificati CELI non hanno scadenza" |
| Where it is published | The CILS FAQ page | The CVCL exams page |
| In the governing regulation? | Not established | No — the Regolamento has no validity article |
| Corroborated on a government domain? | Not found | Yes, IIC Amsterdam (MAECI) |

## The question nobody official answers

Whether an Italian Prefettura or Consolato will accept a ten-year-old B1 certificate for a citizenship application is asked constantly, asserted confidently in both directions online, and addressed by no official source in our research. Not the statute, not the Ministero dell'Interno's filing page, not its *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025, not the consular pages.

So we will not answer it. What we can tell you is the shape of the rule that surrounds it: art. 9.1 of Legge 91/1992 requires the evidence to be produced *all'atto della presentazione dell'istanza* — at the moment of filing — and the Ministry lists the certificate among the mandatory attachments to the electronic application. Those provisions describe *when* the certificate must be in your hands, not *how recently* it must have been earned.

If your certificate is old and your filing matters, put the question to the office that will handle it — your Prefettura if you are in Italy, your Consolato if you are abroad — and get the answer in writing before you decide not to re-sit.

## Do not confuse the certificate with a banked partial result

This is where careless writing produces a real error, so here it is explicitly.

**Capitalizzazione** — banking a passed part so you re-sit only the failed part — is a temporary thing, and it is not the certificate.

- **Perugia.** The Regolamento, art. 1.10, allows a candidate who passed one of the two parts (the whole *parte scritta*, or the *parte orale*) to capitalise that partial result *"per un anno"* — for one year — re-sitting the failed part within that period at 70% of the full fee, at the same level and the same exam type. The regulation does not say what the year runs from. A MAECI cultural-institute page glosses the year as "two exam sessions"; that gloss is the institute's addition and does not appear in the regulation, so use the regulation's wording.
- **Siena.** On the CILS citizenship module there is no capitalizzazione at all: fail one abilità and the whole exam is re-sat. On the standard CILS exams, two Unistrasi pages give different retention windows — 18 months on one, one year from the date of the first exam on the other. Both are official; neither is reliably dated. We report both and resolve neither.
- Separately, Siena's centre manual states that *"Il Riepilogo delle prove d'esame non ha scadenza"* — the summary of exam results does not expire.

A banked part expiring is not your certificate expiring. A certificate, once issued, is what the two universities describe above.

## The timing that actually constrains you

Because the certificate must exist on filing day, the practical constraint is rarely expiry. It is turnaround.

- **Siena.** The candidate-facing results page says results are available after about three months, and that no early assessments are made. A centre-facing manual gives a range from 45 days — for the B1 citizenship module only — up to four months, depending on level and candidate numbers. Those are two different official statements aimed at two different audiences. Neither is a guarantee, and we will not promise you 45 days.
- **Perugia.** The Regolamento, art. 4.1, states that CVCL communicates results approximately 90 working days from the date CVCL receives candidates' scripts — note, from receipt of the papers, not from exam day. Candidates who need proof before the certificate arrives can download a provisional document from their reserved area.

## FAQ

**Has an Italian authority ever said a language certificate expires for citizenship purposes?**
Not on any official source we could reach. The claim circulates in both directions online with no official citation either way.

**If the certificate does not expire, why re-sit?**
There is one situation where it is worth asking the question: if the accepted-bodies list or the procedure changes between your exam and your filing. Our research found the certifying-body list described as four bodies on some official documents and five on others, without an instrument explaining the change.

**Does a provisional CELI document satisfy the filing requirement?**
We do not know. CVCL describes it as a document attesting to passing, downloadable before the certificate arrives. Whether the Prefettura or Consolato accepts it as the mandatory attachment is not addressed by any source we read.

**Can I get a re-mark if I disagree with my result?**
No re-mark procedure is published by either body. Siena allows candidates to ask to view their papers within 30 days of publication, under Law 241/1990, on payment of the access fee. Perugia provides for requests for clarification and for statutory access to records. Neither is an appeal.
