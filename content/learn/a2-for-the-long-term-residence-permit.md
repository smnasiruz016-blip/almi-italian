"Do I need A2 or B1?" is the most common question our readers arrive with, and it has nothing to do with how good anyone's Italian is. It depends entirely on which document you are applying for. B1 belongs to the citizenship application. A2 belongs to the **permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo** — the EU long-term residence permit. Two documents, two levels, two separate bodies of rules that should never be read across.

## The legal chain

The requirement sits in art. 9 of **Decreto Legislativo 25 luglio 1998, n. 286** (the *Testo unico* on immigration), as amended by art. 1, comma 22, lett. i) of **Legge 94/2009**. The implementing rules are in a ministerial decree of **4 June 2010**, published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale on 11 June 2010, whose official title is *"Modalità di svolgimento del test di conoscenza della lingua italiana, previsto dall'articolo 9 del decreto legislativo 25 luglio 1998, n. 286…"*.

Art. 1, comma 1 of that decree states its own purpose: it sets the arrangements for the Italian language test, the passing of which is a condition for issuing the *permesso di soggiorno Ce per soggiornanti di lungo periodo*. Note the decree's older spelling, "Ce"; you will see both "CE" and "UE" in the wild for the same permit.

## Who the decree covers

Art. 1, comma 2 applies the decree to every foreign national requesting the permit under art. 9 of the Testo unico, and to the family members for whom the permit may be requested under comma 1 of that same article — subject to the carve-outs in comma 3.

The Ministero dell'Interno's own page frames the trigger in terms of residence:

> *"Il cittadino straniero che vive legalmente in Italia da più di 5 anni e intende chiedere il permesso CE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo deve sostenere il test disciplinato dal 'Decreto del Ministro dell'Interno 4 giugno 2010'."*
>
> "A foreign citizen who has been living legally in Italy for more than 5 years and intends to apply for the EC long-term residence permit must sit the test governed by the Ministerial Decree of 4 June 2010."

## The level, and an honest caveat about it

The level is **A2**. The Ministero dell'Interno's page states the requirement as *"attestati o titoli che certifichino la conoscenza della lingua italiana a un livello non inferiore all'A2 del Quadro comune di riferimento europeo"* — certificates or qualifications attesting Italian at not below A2 of the CEFR.

The caveat: the article of the 2010 decree that fixes A2 as the threshold for the test itself is one we could not retrieve. The Gazzetta Ufficiale's article-level pages returned HTTP 403 on every attempt. A2 is confirmed from the Ministry's own page; the decree article number that sets it is not something we will state.

## A certificate instead of the test

You do not automatically have to sit the state test. The Ministry's page lists categories who are **non tenuti allo svolgimento del test** (not required to sit the test). One of them is holding a certificate at A2 or above from a recognised body — and here the list differs from the citizenship list. We give these categories as the Ministry describes them, not as a quotation, because the passage came back to us reworded rather than transcribed; since this is the list on which a reader decides to skip the state test, read it on the source at <https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/test-di-conoscenza-della-lingua-italiana> before you act on it:

| Route | Level | Certifying bodies named on the Ministry's page |
|---|---|---|
| EU long-term residence permit | A2 or above | Università per Stranieri di Siena, Università per Stranieri di Perugia, Università degli Studi Roma Tre, Società Dante Alighieri |
| Citizenship (arts. 5 and 9, L. 91/1992) | B1 or above | the five bodies in the Ministry's vademecum of 23 April 2025, plus C.P.I.A. |

The Interior Ministry's A2 page names **four** bodies. Reggio Calabria / Ce.Co.L, which appears on the 2025 citizenship vademecum, does **not** appear on it. We have found no official source saying Ce.Co.L is accepted for the residence-permit route, and we will not tell you it is.

The other categories the Ministry lists as not required to sit the test — again reported as the Ministry describes them, not transcribed from the page — include: completing a course at a *Centro provinciale per l'istruzione degli adulti* with a qualification attesting A2; recognition at A2 within the credits of the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement); holding an Italian secondary-school diploma, or attending or holding an Italian university degree, doctorate or master's; and certain admitted categories such as *dirigente*, highly qualified worker, university professor, researcher, translator or interpreter, and officially accredited foreign correspondent journalist.

Separately from those, the decree exempts two groups outright. Those, and the citizenship exemptions, are on our exemptions page.

## Where A2 exams come from

This is exam-provider information, not law. Perugia's CVCL links its **CELI 1 i (A2)** exam to the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* on its immigrati page; the immigrati series is printed on the official calendar with an "i" suffix. Siena's CILS calendar for 2026 lists A2 sittings, including a module the calendar labels "A2 integrazione" — no official page we read defines what distinguishes that module, so we do not describe it. Choose the exam on the awarding body's own current page, and check the level printed on the certificate matches what the Ministry's list asks for.

## FAQ

**Does the A2 requirement apply when I renew the permit, or only for the first issue?**
The decree speaks of *"il rilascio"* — the issuing. Whether it reaches renewals is not addressed on any source we could read. Check the current official page before assuming either way.

**If I hold the EU long-term permit, do I still need B1 for citizenship?**
Holders of that permit are among the categories relieved of the obligation under art. 9.1. There is a live disagreement between ministries about whether a date qualifier attaches to that exemption, so read our exemptions page before relying on it.

**Can I use one certificate for both procedures?**
A certificate at B1 or above from a body on both lists is above the A2 threshold and at or above the B1 threshold. The lists and the checking are done separately by different offices, so confirm with each.
