There is one point on the official speaking mark scheme that our practice tool cannot see, and we would rather you heard it here than discovered it later. Siena awards up to one point for **pronuncia e intonazione** (pronunciation and intonation). Our feedback works from a transcript of what you said, so it can assess your words and your grammar but not the sound of them. That point is outside its reach, and no amount of confident-sounding output from us would change it.

Everything else about this section is published, and worth knowing precisely.

## What happens in the room

The syllabus gives one spoken test of about ten minutes, in two parts: a personal presentation of roughly a minute, then a dialogue of two to three minutes. Siena's page on how CILS exams are run describes the spoken exam as "un colloquio fra il candidato e il somministratore e un monologo" (a conversation between the candidate and the administrator, and a monologue), notes that the speaking tests are usually held in the afternoon, and states that they are recorded.

The recording matters for two reasons. It is why the marking can happen centrally in Siena rather than in the room with you, and it is why the examiner in front of you is not the person deciding your score.

## The four criteria and their weights

| Criterion (Italian) | English | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Efficacia comunicativa | Communicative effectiveness | 4 |
| Correttezza morfosintattica | Morphosyntactic accuracy | 4 |
| Adeguatezza e ricchezza lessicale | Lexical appropriateness and range | 3 |
| Pronuncia e intonazione | Pronunciation and intonation | 1 |
| **Total** | | **12** |

Two things stand out when you compare this with the written task. First, **efficacia comunicativa is worth 4 here rather than 3** — getting the message across counts for more when you are speaking. Second, there is no separate criterion for style or for spelling; pronunciation takes the single point instead, "pronuncia e intonazione: fino a punti 1" (up to 1 point).

That single point is often over-weighted in candidates' anxiety. Eight of the twelve points ride on saying something that works and saying it accurately. An accent, on this scale, costs at most one point — and it is not a criterion for sounding Italian, but for being understood and using intonation that carries meaning.

## Preparing for a section a transcript cannot capture

Because our tool reads what you said rather than hearing it, use it for the eleven points it can see: whether you covered the task, whether the grammar held up under time pressure, whether your vocabulary widened beyond the first word that came to mind. For the twelfth point, and for fluency under real conversational pressure, you need a human listener or a recording of yourself — practices we would rather recommend than pretend to replace.

Recording yourself has a second benefit that no marking scheme captures: the one-minute presentation is the only part of the exam you can rehearse in advance, because it is about you.

## Questions readers ask

**Who am I talking to?**
The somministratore, the person administering the exam at your centre. They conduct the conversation; the Centro CILS in Siena marks the recording.

**Is the exam in the afternoon?**
Siena's exam-conduct page says the spoken tests usually take place in the afternoon. Your centre confirms the actual timing.

**Will a strong accent fail me?**
Pronunciation and intonation are worth one point out of twelve. We cannot tell you how examiners apply that point, because the band descriptors are not published, but its weight is small and it is published.

**Can I ask for the question to be repeated?**
Nothing we could verify addresses this. Ask your centre before the exam rather than relying on assumption.

**Can I resit only the speaking test if it goes badly?**
No. This module does not allow capitalizzazione, so a failure in any skill means retaking the entire exam in the next session.
