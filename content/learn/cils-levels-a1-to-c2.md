Six levels, six certificates, and no ladder you are obliged to climb in order. That is the shape of CILS, and the names on the certificates are not the ones most people expect, because the Università per Stranieri di Siena numbers its middle levels rather than lettering them.

## The ladder

Siena states that CILS "copre tutti i sei livelli di competenza linguistico-comunicativa individuati dal Quadro Comune Europeo di Riferimento per le Lingue" (covers all six levels of communicative language competence identified by the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages). It sorts them into three bands:

| Band (Siena's grouping) | Official CILS name | CEFR level |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | A1 | A1 |
| Basic | A2 | A2 |
| Autonomous | CILS UNO-B1 | B1 |
| Autonomous | CILS DUE-B2 | B2 |
| Competent | CILS TRE-C1 | C1 |
| Competent | CILS QUATTRO-C2 | C2 |

The two lowest levels keep their CEFR letters. From B1 upward, the CILS name leads with an Italian ordinal: UNO (one), DUE (two), TRE (three), QUATTRO (four). A certificate reading "CILS DUE-B2" is a B2 certificate; the "DUE" is not a second attempt or a second part.

## Levels are self-contained

"Ogni livello CILS è autonomo e completo" (every CILS level is autonomous and complete), the university's level page says. Practically, that means three things. You may enter at whichever level matches your competence, without holding the one below. Passing a level is a finished result, not a credit towards the next. And there is no institutional path you must have followed first: Siena requires no particular educational qualifications to sit a CILS exam and sets no age limit.

Which level you should sit is therefore a question about your Italian and about what a receiving authority requires — not about sequence. For citizenship, Siena's own pages are unambiguous that B1 is the relevant level; what a prefettura or consulate will accept as evidence is a legal matter and belongs on our citizenship pages, not here.

## Modules inside the levels

Several levels are not single exams. Siena's UNO-B1 page states that the level contains three modules: B1 standard, B1 A and B1 C. The exam calendar likewise distinguishes an A2 standard from an A2 integrazione module, and a B2 standard from a B2 adolescenti (adolescents) module.

Two cautions matter here. First, the calendar's B1 C is the citizenship module — a different, shorter exam with its own rules, covered in its own section of this site. Second, the calendar's B1 A is a separate module and is not the citizenship one; no official page we could reach defines what B1 A is, so we will not describe it. If a school tells you B1 A is the citizenship exam, that is not what the calendar says.

## When each level is offered

Siena runs six sessions a year and publishes, for each, which levels are on offer. The 2026 calendar reads as follows.

| 2026 sitting | Levels named on the calendar | Registration deadline |
|---|---|---|
| 19 February | A2 integrazione, B1 C | 19 January |
| 14 April | A2 standard, A2 integrazione, B1 A, B1 C, B2 standard, B2 adolescenti, C1 | 14 March |
| 16 June | all levels | 7 May |
| 22 July | A2 integrazione, B1 C | 22 June |
| 21 October | A2 standard, A2 integrazione, B1 C | 21 September |
| 3 December | all levels | 24 October |

The consequence is worth stating plainly: if you want A1, C1 or C2, your choice of dates is far narrower than six a year, and in 2026 the sessions listed as covering all levels are June and December. The 2027 calendar lists 18 February, 8 April, 10 June, 22 July, 21 October and 2 December, with deadlines of 18 January, 8 March, 1 May, 22 June, 21 September and 23 October.

Note the rhythm of the deadlines. Most fall about a month before the sitting; June and December fall five to six weeks or more before. That is an observation about the two published calendars rather than a rule Siena states, so read the calendar for your own year rather than counting back a month.

## Practicalities that apply across the ladder

You register through an exam centre — in Italy or abroad — not centrally. Exams take place at centres that have signed a convention with Siena; abroad, Italian Cultural Institutes and other partners coordinate them locally. Every paper, at every level, is marked centrally at the Centro CILS in Siena, which also wrote the assessment criteria.

On price, only one figure is published by the university for candidates: 100 euros for the B1 citizenship module, in Italy and abroad. For A1, A2, standard B1, B2, C1 and C2, no current official fee was available to us, and the honest answer is to ask the centre where you plan to sit.

On expiry, Siena's FAQ says the certificate "attualmente non ha una scadenza" (currently has no expiry). The word "attualmente" is the university's, and we keep it.

## Questions people actually ask

**Is CILS UNO-B1 the same as CEFR B1?**
Yes — UNO-B1 is Siena's name for its B1 certification. The ordinal is part of the brand name, not a sub-level.

**Can I sit two levels in one session?**
Not something any official page we read addresses. Ask your centre, which handles registration and scheduling.

**If I fail, must I drop a level?**
No. Levels are autonomous, and the standard exams allow capitalizzazione, meaning a re-sit of the failed skill rather than the whole exam. The citizenship module is the exception, and that exception is consequential.

**Does CELI use the same names?**
No. CELI is a different exam from a different university, with its own level names and its own scoring, and we deliberately keep the two apart — see our CELI section.
