This is our own product, so we owe you the plainest page on the site. What follows is what our feedback is, what it is not, and the one thing it structurally cannot see.

## What the estimate is

It is a criteria-based estimate, scored against the criteria the Università per Stranieri di Siena publishes for CILS B1 Cittadinanza. We did not invent a scale. Siena's criteria document divides the *produzione scritta* into five named criteria totalling 12 points, and the *produzione orale* into four named criteria totalling 12 points. Our feedback assigns a figure to those same criteria, with those same maxima, and shows you the breakdown rather than a single number.

That is the whole design intention: when you finish a practice task you should be able to see which of Siena's published criteria you are weakest on, in Siena's own terms, and watch that change over a few weeks.

## The thing we cannot see

Siena's criteria list, for the oral, *"pronuncia e intonazione: fino a punti 1"* — pronunciation and intonation, up to 1 point. Our speaking feedback works from a transcript of what you said. A transcript does not carry sound. It cannot tell us how you pronounced a word, where you put the stress, whether your intonation turned a statement into a question, or whether a real examiner would have had to ask you to repeat yourself.

So we do not score that criterion. We show it as **not assessed**, and we do not fold a guess into your total. One point out of twelve is small, but a guessed point and a measured point look identical on a screen, and you would plan your revision around both.

Two consequences you should hold on to. First, our oral estimate speaks to 11 of the 12 published points, not 12. Second, a transcript can flatter: a passage that reads smoothly may have been hard to follow aloud, and a mis-transcribed word can look like a vocabulary error that you never made. For pronunciation you need a human ear, a conversation partner, or your own recordings played back the following day. Nothing on this site replaces that.

## What the estimate is not

**It is not an official result, and it is never a prediction of one.** CILS papers are marked centrally at the Centro CILS in Siena, which wrote the criteria; CELI written papers are marked centrally at CVCL in Perugia by trained examiners. We are in neither building and have no relationship with either university. Their criteria are public documents; using a public document is not endorsement by its author.

**We will never tell you that you would have passed.** For the Cittadinanza module there is nothing to predict against, because Siena publishes no pass mark for it — see below.

**Our thresholds are ours.** The {{CILS_B1C_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} floor per skill and the {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} total you see in the product are figures we derived from the official standard CILS UNO–B1 rule: {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points, 55 to pass, minimum {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} per skill. Eleven out of twenty is 55 per cent; 55 per cent of 12 is 6.6, rounded up to 7; four skills at 7 is {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}}. **Siena publishes no pass mark and no per-skill minimum for the B1 Cittadinanza module at all.** Our code says the same thing our pages do.

**We do not resolve the conflict in the official record, either.** Siena's criteria document for this module totals {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} points. An Italian government information sheet describes both B1 exams as marked out of 100, with 55 to pass and a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} per skill. One plausible reading is that raw marks are rescaled for reporting — but no official document says so, so we report both figures and leave them unreconciled.

## If you are preparing for CELI

Our criteria-based estimate is built on Siena's weights for a Siena exam. It carries no information about Perugia's.

CELI publishes its own thresholds, and they are not interchangeable with anything above: for CELI 2 (B1), a minimum of {{CELI_DUE_WRITTEN_MIN}} in the written part, 22 in the oral, and an overall score in the range 94 to 160. For the CELI 2 i (B1) Cittadinanza module, the written and oral minima are the same 72 and 22, but the overall minimum is 92, not 94. Two different exams, two separately-set cut-offs, and no official statement explaining the two-point difference. Never average them, and never read a CILS estimate onto either.

## What it is genuinely good for

A consistent yardstick, available whenever you are. It applies the same criteria to the same kind of task every time, so the comparison between last Tuesday's attempt and this Tuesday's is meaningful even though the estimate itself is not official. It answers "which criterion am I losing on" and "has that moved" — which is what a practice tool should answer, and what a single score cannot.

## Questions we get

**If you give me 9 out of {{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}}, would Siena?**
Unknown, and we will not pretend otherwise. Ours is an estimate against published criteria by a third party; theirs is a mark by the body that owns the exam.

**Why not just guess the pronunciation point so the total looks complete?**
Because a complete-looking total is precisely the problem. You would read 10/12 and revise as though pronunciation had been checked. It has not been.

**Are you affiliated with Siena or Perugia?**
No. We use their published criteria and we link their documents so you can read them yourself.

**Should I use anything else?**
Yes. Unistrasi publishes B1 Cittadinanza preparation material at no charge — syllabus, prototype booklet, audio, transcriptions, keys and the criteria document — and for pronunciation you need a person, not a transcript.
