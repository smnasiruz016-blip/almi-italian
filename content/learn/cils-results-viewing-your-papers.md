You sat the exam, weeks have gone by, and the results page still shows nothing. Before you email anyone, it is worth knowing that two official Unistrasi documents describe the timescale differently — and that neither of them is a promise you can plan a filing deadline around.

## What Siena tells candidates

The candidate-facing results page is unambiguous and short: "I risultati degli esami saranno disponibili dopo circa 3 mesi. Non si eseguono valutazioni anticipate." (Exam results will be available after about 3 months. Early assessments are not carried out.)

The second sentence is doing real work. There is no expedited route, no priority handling for someone with an application deadline, and no marking-in-advance for a candidate who explains why they are in a hurry.

## The other figure, and why we will not simplify it

Siena's manual for exam centres puts it differently, describing publication "in un arco di tempo che può variare da 45 giorni (solo per il livello B1 modulo Cittadinanza) ai 4 mesi, a seconda dei livelli e del numero dei candidati della sessione" (over a period that may vary from 45 days — only for the B1 Cittadinanza module — to 4 months, depending on the levels and the number of candidates in the session).

| Source | Audience | Timescale given |
|---|---|---|
| Risultati degli esami page | candidates | about 3 months |
| Istruzioni per le sedi di esame | exam centres | 45 days to 4 months, depending on level and candidate numbers |

Read the second one carefully. Forty-five days is the fast end of a range, it is named for one module only, and it appears in a document written for centres rather than for candidates. Anyone who tells you CILS results arrive in 45 days is quoting the best case in someone else's manual. Plan on the candidate-facing figure, treat anything faster as luck, and never build an immigration deadline on either number.

## How to check your result

Results are published on the university's site and looked up individually. You will need your matricola (the candidate registration number assigned at registration) and your date of birth; the lookup runs through the "IL MIO RISULTATO" service. Keep the matricola somewhere safe on exam day — without it the lookup does not work, and it is not something your memory will reconstruct.

Marking is central, not local: all exam papers are assessed at the Centro CILS, which also wrote the assessment criteria. Your exam centre administers and invigilates; it does not mark, and it cannot tell you a result before publication.

## Collecting the certificate

The certificate is not posted to you as a matter of course. Siena's instruction is direct: "Per il ritiro del certificato devi rivolgerti alla Sede in cui hai sostenuto l'esame" — to collect the certificate you must apply to the centre where you sat the exam. If you have moved, changed country, or sat the exam while travelling, that is a practical problem worth raising with the centre early; no official page we could read describes an alternative delivery arrangement.

On expiry, the university's FAQ says the certificate attualmente (currently) has no expiry date. We keep the qualifier because Siena uses it: the university is describing the present position, not making a permanent commitment. Siena also states that the CILS certification is recognised internationally in both academic and professional contexts.

## Viewing your papers — what it is, and what it is not

There is a documented right of access. Within 30 days of the publication of results, a candidate may ask to view their own exam papers by writing to trasparenzacertificazioni@unistrasi.it, enclosing identification and personal details together with the matricola, and paying the access fee provided for under Law 241/90 (the Italian statute on access to administrative documents).

Two things follow from that, and both matter.

First, the clock is short and it starts at publication, not at the exam. Thirty days after results appear is a narrow window for someone who is waiting on a certificate, travelling, or simply not checking the site daily. If you think you may want to see your papers, check the publication date rather than waiting for a certificate to arrive.

Second, access is not a re-mark. It lets you look at what you submitted and how it was assessed; nothing in the procedure obliges anyone to reconsider the score. We searched the official pages for an appeal route and found none: no official re-mark procedure is published. That is a statement about what Siena publishes, not a claim that no internal process exists anywhere — if it matters to you, ask the Centro CILS what your options are, in writing.

## Questions people actually ask

**Can I get my result faster if my citizenship or permit deadline is close?**
No. The results page states that early assessments are not carried out. Build the waiting time into your plan instead: work back from your filing date, through certificate collection, through publication, to the sitting.

**Can my exam centre tell me informally how I did?**
Papers are marked centrally at the Centro CILS. The centre does not hold your marks.

**Is there any way to appeal a mark?**
No official re-mark procedure is published. What is published is the right to view your papers within 30 days, under Law 241/90, on payment of the access fee.

**What does viewing my papers cost?**
Siena's page refers to the access fee under Law 241/90 without publishing an amount where we could see it. Ask when you write to trasparenzacertificazioni@unistrasi.it.
