Start with a disclaimer that matters more than anything below it. **We have no official data on why citizenship applications are refused.** No Italian government source we could reach publishes refusal statistics, a list of common defects, or guidance to caseworkers on rejecting language evidence. Anyone offering you a ranked list of "top refusal reasons" is guessing, and so would we be.

What does exist is a published requirement, written in specific terms, with specific things it says a file must contain. Below are eight ways a language file fails to match that requirement as written. Each is derived from an official document, and each is stated as a mismatch with the rule — not as a claim about what caseworkers actually do.

The Ministry itself frames the check as a serious one. Its *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025 says:

> *"Il controllo del possesso della certificazione linguistica risulta di rilevante importanza ai fini della concessione della cittadinanza italiana."*
> "The check on possession of the language certification is of considerable importance for the purposes of granting Italian citizenship."

## Eight mismatches with the rule as published

### 1. The certificate is below B1

The statute and every restatement of it use the same formula: *non inferiore al livello B1* — not below B1. An A2 certificate satisfies the residence-permit rule under a different instrument entirely. It does not satisfy art. 9.1.

### 2. The certificate comes from a body not on the Ministry's list

The *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025 names five certifying bodies plus the CPIA network — reported here as our researcher read that document, not transcribed from it, because the passage came back to us reworded: Università per Stranieri di Siena (CILS); Università per Stranieri di Perugia (CELI); Università degli Studi Roma Tre (CERT.IT); Società Dante Alighieri (PLIDA); Università per stranieri "Dante Alighieri" di Reggio Calabria; and the *Centri provinciali per l'istruzione degli adulti* (provincial adult-education centres). Check the names against the vademecum itself at <https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/sites/default/files/2025-06/vademecum_23_aprile_2025.pdf>. A certificate from anywhere else is not on that list.

### 3. Your certificate is from the fifth body, and the page in front of your official is the older one

Other Ministry documents still online list only **four** bodies, without Reggio Calabria: a Prefettura-hosted PDF dated June 2024, and MAECI's central certifications page, which describes four bodies grouped in the CLIQ association. The five-body list is the April 2025 Vademecum and the MAECI consular pages.

We could not find the decree or ministerial act that added the fifth body, so we cannot tell you when the list changed or that the older documents are simply out of date. If you hold a Reggio Calabria certificate, raise it with your Prefettura or Consolato in writing before filing.

### 4. The Italian qualification was not obtained in Italian, or is below lower-secondary level

An Italian school or university qualification is an alternative to a certificate — but the Vademecum sets two conditions. The qualification must come from a public or state-recognised institution recognised by MIM, MUR or MAECI, *"a partire dalla scuola secondaria di primo grado"* (from lower secondary school upward), and:

> *"Il titolo di studio deve essere conseguito necessariamente in lingua italiana."*
> "The qualification must necessarily have been obtained in the Italian language."

A degree from an Italian university taught in English does not, on that wording, do the job.

### 5. The certificate did not exist on the day of filing

Art. 9.1 requires the evidence *all'atto della presentazione dell'istanza* — at the moment the application is lodged. A certificate issued a fortnight after filing is a certificate you did not have when the rule required you to have one.

### 6. The certificate was not attached to the electronic application

The Ministry's filing page lists the language certification among the mandatory attachments. The rest of that list reached us as a paraphrase of the page rather than a transcription, so we report it rather than reproduce it, and we cannot tell you it is exhaustive: alongside the certificate it names an identity document, birth and criminal-record certificates from the country of origin, the receipt for the €250 payment, and the electronic *marca da bollo* details for filings made in Italy. On the €250: that figure is recorded in a Consolato Generale d'Italia notice of **January 2019** and repeated in the Ministry's current document list — re-check the amount on the Ministry's page before you pay. Read the list yourself at <https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/cittadinanza-invia-la-tua-domanda>.

### 7. The disability exemption is evidenced by a GP

Corte costituzionale sentenza n. 25/2025 struck down art. 9.1 in part for failing to exempt applicants with serious language-learning impairments. The Ministry implemented it, and its Vademecum requires the impairment to be attested *"mediante certificazione rilasciata dalla struttura sanitaria pubblica"* — by a public health facility. Our reading of the same document records it as noting that a certificate from a *medico di base* (general practitioner) is excluded; that came back to us reworded rather than transcribed, so we report it rather than state it as a rule. If your evidence is a GP's letter, ask your Prefettura what they will accept before you file.

Beyond "struttura sanitaria pubblica", we have no verified detail on the required format or issuing body.

### 8. You relied on the EU long-term permit exemption where sources disagree

The Interior Ministry's filing page exempts holders of the *permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo* without qualification. MAECI consular pages condition the same exemption on a permit **issued by Italian authorities after 9 December 2010**. That qualifier does not appear in the statutory text or on the Interior Ministry pages we read.

If your permit is older than that date, or was issued by another member state, this is unresolved and it decides your file. Confirm in writing.

## A ninth, procedural: filing through the wrong door

The Ministry's current instruction names **cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it**, reached with SPID or CIE, or by registering if you live abroad. Legacy "Portale ALI" addresses under `portaleservizi.dlci.interno.it/AliCittadinanza` still resolve and still dominate search results and third-party guides. We could not establish whether they redirect, run in parallel, or are deprecated. Use the address the Ministry publishes today.

## What we cannot tell you about refusals

- **How to appeal a refusal on language grounds.** No source we read describes a route.
- **What happens to an application already pending when a rule changes.** No transitional provisions were addressed by any source we read.
- **Whether a generic B1 module from a listed body is treated differently from a citizenship-branded one.** The government texts specify a level and a list of bodies, not modules. Nothing official we found rules either way.
- **How often any of this actually happens.** See the first paragraph.

## FAQ

**Is a certificate from a private language school ever enough?**
Not on the Ministry's list. The list names universities, the Dante Alighieri society and the CPIA network.

**Does a CPIA qualification work the same way as a university certificate?**
Both the June 2024 Prefettura document and the April 2025 Vademecum include CPIA, but we could not transcribe the Vademecum's framing in full — whether it treats CPIA as a certifying body or as an alternative *titolo di studio*. We are not stating the mechanism.

**My certificate is fifteen years old. Is that a problem?**
No official source we could reach addresses the age of a language certificate for citizenship purposes. See our page on certificate validity.

**Can I file now and add the certificate later?**
Art. 9.1 says the evidence is produced at the moment of filing. We found no official description of a later-supply route.
