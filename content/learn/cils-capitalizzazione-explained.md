Two pages on the same university's website give two different answers to the same question, and the question is one that decides whether a candidate has to re-sit an entire exam. How long does a passed CILS skill stay banked? One official Unistrasi page says 18 months. Another official Unistrasi document says one year. We are not going to pretend that is settled.

## What capitalizzazione means

Capitalizzazione (literally "capitalisation", in practice the banking of a passed section) is the mechanism by which a candidate who passes some skills and fails others keeps the passes and re-sits only the failures at a later session. CILS exams are assessed skill by skill — the abilità (skills) such as listening, reading, writing and speaking — and it is at that level that banking operates: you re-sit a failed skill, not an individual question or task.

An Italian government information sheet describes the standard B1 arrangement as "Con capitalizzazione – se l'esame non è superato in qualche abilità, si potrà sostenere di nuovo l'esame solo nella parte non sufficiente" (with capitalisation — if the exam is not passed in some skill, the exam may be re-sat only in the part that was insufficient). Unistrasi's own assessment page describes the same mechanism.

## The disagreement about the window

Here is the conflict in full, with both sources named.

| Source | What it says | Effective window |
|---|---|---|
| Unistrasi, "La valutazione" page | "Entro 18 mesi… il candidato può presentarsi allo stesso esame e sostenere solo le abilità che non ha superato" — within 18 months the candidate may present themselves for the same exam and sit only the skills not passed | 18 months |
| Unistrasi, Linee guida CILS (PDF) | passed skills remain valid "un anno dalla data del primo esame sostenuto" — one year from the date of the first exam sat | 12 months |

Both are on unistrasi.it. Neither carries a reliable date that would let us say which supersedes the other. We are reporting the conflict rather than resolving it, because resolving it would mean guessing on a point where a wrong guess costs somebody a whole exam.

Our own planning judgement — clearly ours, not a rule from Siena — is that a candidate who plans against the shorter of the two windows is protected either way, and that anyone whose re-sit would fall between the twelve-month and eighteen-month marks should write to the Centro CILS or ask their exam centre in writing before relying on a banked skill. Note also that results are published some time after the sitting, which eats into whichever window applies: with six sessions a year, the number of realistic re-sit slots inside a twelve-month window is smaller than it first looks.

## The exclusion that matters most: B1 Cittadinanza

Capitalizzazione is not available for the CILS B1 citizenship module. Unistrasi's page for that exam states it directly, and an Italian government information sheet says the same in its comparison of the two B1 exams: for the citizenship module, if the exam is not passed in some skill, that skill cannot be recovered and the whole exam must be sat again at the next session.

This is the single most consequential difference between the citizenship module and the standard exams, and it is verified twice over on official sources. A candidate who passes listening, reading and writing but falls short in speaking re-sits everything. If you are sitting the citizenship module, nothing on this page about banked skills applies to you; our dedicated page on that exclusion is the one to read.

| Exam | Capitalizzazione |
|---|---|
| Standard CILS UNO-B1 | allowed |
| CILS B1 citizenship module | not allowed |

## Practical questions this raises

Re-sitting means registering again through an exam centre for a later session. Siena runs six sessions a year, though not every level is offered at every session, so a partial re-sit still depends on the calendar carrying your level.

What a partial re-sit costs, we cannot tell you. Unistrasi publishes a candidate-facing figure only for the citizenship module. There is no published statement that a banked-skill re-sit is charged at a reduced rate, or at the full rate, and inventing one would be worse than saying so.

## Questions people actually ask

**Does capitalizzazione apply to every CILS level?**
We verified it explicitly for the standard B1 exam, on a government information sheet, and Unistrasi's assessment page describes the mechanism in general terms. We did not confirm that every level and every module works identically, and the citizenship module is a documented exception. Check the guidance for the specific level you are sitting.

**Is a banked skill recorded on a certificate?**
Not addressed on any official page we read. What Siena documents is the entitlement to re-sit only the failed skills, not how the interim state is recorded.

**Which window should I tell my exam centre applies?**
Neither, on your own authority. Show them both and ask them to confirm with the Centro CILS. The value of knowing about a contradiction is that you can ask a precise question, not that you can pick the answer you prefer.

**Does CELI work the same way?**
CELI is a different exam run by a different university, and its own rules on banking are covered on our CELI page. Do not carry a CILS rule across to a CELI exam or the reverse.
