Where the clock starts is the part everyone gets wrong. The CELI regulation does not promise results a set number of days after you sit the exam. It counts from the day Perugia receives your papers:

> "Il CVCL provvede alla comunicazione dei risultati all'incirca 90 giorni lavorativi a partire dalla data di ricezione degli elaborati dei candidati da parte del CVCL"
> *CVCL communicates results approximately 90 working days from the date CVCL receives candidates' scripts.*
> — Regolamento degli esami CELI, art. 4.1

Three words in that sentence do real work. *All'incirca* means approximately. *Giorni lavorativi* means working days, not calendar days. And the start date is CVCL's receipt of the scripts, which depends on how quickly your centre despatches them — a step no published document times.

The same article adds an explicit exception: timings may differ where further checks are needed after serious irregularities are detected on the answer sheets. So this is a stated norm with a stated escape clause, not a service guarantee, and we will not present it as one. No official source states the typical gap between exam day and results publication.

## What you can and cannot know before then

The oral commission assesses your performance in the room and, per the regulation, does not tell you the score obtained. There is no partial or early release: the letter grade and the numbers arrive together, at the end.

## Checking your result

Candidates access their own result online through the reserved area of the CVCL site, using their *numero di matricola* (candidate registration number) and their date of birth. The regulation frames this as a privacy measure — nobody sees anyone else's outcome. Keep the matricola number from your enrolment paperwork; without it the online route does not work.

Component-level detail is not online. The regulation states that results are also available at the centre where the exam was held, including the scores obtained in the individual components. If you want to know whether it was the listening or the writing that cost you the written part — the question that actually matters when you are deciding what to re-sit — the centre is where to ask.

## The certificate itself

Certificates are issued in electronic format by CVCL, which sends them to the exam centre; the centre then distributes them. You do not receive one directly from Perugia.

If you need proof before the certificate reaches you, the regulation provides for it: candidates who need a provisional document attesting that they passed can download it from their reserved area. That matters for anyone working to a filing deadline, and it is a genuinely useful provision that is not widely known.

CVCL states that CELI certificates have no expiry. Note, though, that the no-expiry statement appears on the CVCL website and not in the Regolamento, which contains no article on validity at all.

## If you disagree with the result

Here the honest answer is narrower than most guides suggest. The regulation's article on this is headed *Richieste di chiarimenti* — requests for clarification — not *ricorso* or *riesame*:

> "Il candidato può chiedere chiarimenti in merito ai risultati rivolgendosi all'indirizzo certificazione@unistrapg.it."
> *The candidate may request clarification about the results by writing to certificazione@unistrapg.it.*
> — Regolamento degli esami CELI, art. 4.3

The same article records the candidate's right of access to records, exercised through the university's central administration under Italian administrative-transparency law: Law 241/1990 and following amendments, DPR 184/2006 and Legislative Decree 196/2003.

Clarification and access to documents are not a re-mark. No re-marking or appeal procedure is published in the CELI regulation. Write "no re-mark procedure is published" rather than "you can appeal", because the second is not something the regulation supports.

It is worth knowing that marking of the written part is centralised at CVCL, according to criteria and score scales that CVCL itself publishes, and carried out by examiners trained for the work. Your centre does not mark your paper and cannot change your score.

## Planning around the wait

If your result feeds an immigration file, build the timeline backwards and leave slack.

- The statute requires language evidence to be produced **at the moment the application is filed**, not added later, so the certificate has to exist before you file.
- Results are counted in working days from CVCL's receipt of scripts, with an explicit exception for further checks.
- The certificate travels CVCL → centre → you, and each hop takes time nobody publishes.
- The downloadable provisional document exists precisely for the gap between passing and holding the certificate — but whether any given office accepts it in place of the certificate is a question for that office, not for us.

Do not book a session on the assumption that a result will land by a particular date.

## Questions

**Is 90 working days a promise?**
No. The regulation says *approximately*, in working days, from CVCL's receipt of the scripts, and allows for longer where further checks are required.

**Can I get my result faster if I explain why I need it?**
Nothing in the published regulation provides for that.

**Where do I see my score for each part?**
At the exam centre. The online area gives you your result; the centre holds the component scores.

**What if my certificate is lost between Perugia and my centre?**
Not addressed in what CVCL publishes. Contact the centre first, then certificazione@unistrapg.it.
