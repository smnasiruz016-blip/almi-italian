You walked out of the oral feeling fine and the written result came back short. Does that mean the whole exam again, from the beginning, at full price? Under the CELI regulation, no — provided you act inside a window and inside four conditions. The mechanism is called *capitalizzazione*, literally the capitalising or banking of a partial result.

## What the regulation grants

Article 1.10 of the Regolamento degli esami CELI covers it. A candidate who failed the written part but passed the oral, or failed the oral but passed the written, may keep the passed half:

> "possono capitalizzare per un anno il risultato parziale ottenuto… pagando il 70% della tassa complessiva"
> *may capitalise the partial result obtained for one year… paying 70% of the total fee.*
> — Regolamento degli esami CELI, art. 1.10

The re-sit is not open-ended in scope, either:

> "Si specifica che tale prova dovrà riferirsi al medesimo livello e tipologia d'esame sostenuto in precedenza."
> *It is specified that such a test must refer to the same level and type of exam previously taken.*
> — same article

## The four conditions, set out plainly

| Condition | What the regulation says |
|---|---|
| Direction | Works both ways: failed written with passed oral, or failed oral with passed written |
| Duration | One year — *per un anno* |
| Cost | 70 per cent of the full exam fee |
| Scope | The re-sit must be at the same level **and** the same exam type |

That last condition is the one candidates trip over. *Tipologia d'esame* is exam type, not just level. On the plain reading of the wording, a partial result from a generic CELI 2 sitting does not carry into a CELI 2 i cittadinanza sitting, even though both are B1. If you intend to bank a part, book the same exam again.

## What counts as one "part"

This is where CELI's architecture decides the answer. The *parte scritta* is not a single skill. The regulation defines it as reading comprehension, written production, *Competenza Linguistica* (from CELI 3 upwards) and listening comprehension, taken together. The *parte orale* is the other unit.

So capitalizzazione operates on two aggregate units, not on four skills. If you failed the written part because the listening went badly, the plain reading of the regulation is that you re-sit the written part as a block — reading, writing and listening again. No official source states that as an explicit prohibition on retaking a single component, but nothing supports it either, and the regulation's own definition of the written part is against it.

## One year — but one year from what?

The regulation says *per un anno* and stops there. It does not say whether the year runs from the exam date, from the date results are published, or from the session. We could not find any official clarification, and we are not going to invent one. If you are near the edge of the window, ask your centre and ask CVCL in writing before you assume you are inside it.

There is a competing gloss worth knowing about. A MAECI page — the Italian Cultural Institute in Amsterdam — describes the partial result as remaining valid for one year, framed as two exam sittings. That "two sessions" framing does not appear in the Regolamento, and the published CELI calendar shows more than two sittings a year at B1. We report both and follow the Regolamento's own wording: one year, stated as a period of time, not as a count of sessions.

## The certificate does not expire; the banked half does

Two different clocks, easy to confuse and expensive to confuse. CVCL states that CELI certificates have no expiry. That is about the certificate you receive after passing. The one-year limit in article 1.10 is about a partial result held between sittings. A page that blurs them produces a factual error; so does a reader who does.

## Where CELI and CILS genuinely differ

This is a real structural difference, and it is worth stating precisely because the two exams are so often described as interchangeable.

CELI, per the Regolamento, banks one of **two aggregate parts** — everything written, or the oral — for one year, at 70 per cent of the fee, at the same level and exam type.

CILS is a different certification from a different university, the Università per Stranieri di Siena, and it works at the level of individual *abilità* (skills), tracked by the candidate's *numero di matricola*. Crucially, Siena states that its B1 Cittadinanza module allows no capitalizzazione at all: fail one skill there and the entire exam is taken again at the next session. We could not find an official Siena statement of the retention period or re-sit cost for the modules that do allow it, so the comparison cannot be completed from official sources — and we are not completing it from a language school's blog.

Do not carry either institution's figures across to the other.

## Questions

**Does capitalizzazione apply to the citizenship module, CELI 2 i?**
Unresolved. Article 1.10 sits in the general regulation and its language reads as covering CELI exams generally, but no source explicitly confirms the mechanism operates for the immigrant-series citizenship exam. Ask your centre.

**Can I use it twice, or chain it?**
No official statement found either way.

**Must the re-sit be at the same centre?**
The regulation specifies level and exam type only. Notably, the separate article covering absence through illness does require the same centre — which suggests the omission in article 1.10 may be deliberate. Not resolved; ask.

**Is the 70 per cent calculated on the fee I paid or the current one?**
Not specified. Since CVCL publishes no fee at all and centres collect it, this is a question for your centre.
