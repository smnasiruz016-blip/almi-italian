Timing is the part people get wrong. The language certificate is not a document you send in later when someone asks for it. Art. 9.1 of Law 91/1992 requires applicants *"all'atto della presentazione dell'istanza, ad attestare…"* — at the moment of filing the application, to attest. If the certificate is not in hand when you file, you are not filing a complete application. Everything below follows from that one clause.

## Where the application is filed

The Ministero dell'Interno's current instruction names one address:

> *"Il richiedente la cittadinanza italiana accede tramite SPID o CIE, ovvero si registra sul portale informatico se residente all'estero, al seguente indirizzo: <https://cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it>"*
>
> "The applicant for Italian citizenship logs in via SPID or CIE, or registers on the online portal if resident abroad, at the following address: https://cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it"

**SPID** is the Italian public digital identity system; **CIE** is the *carta d'identità elettronica* (electronic identity card). Applicants resident abroad register on the portal instead.

A warning about search results. The older "Portale ALI" addresses under `portaleservizi.dlci.interno.it/AliCittadinanza` still dominate search engines and third-party guides, and those links still resolve. The Ministry's current page names `cittadinanza.dlci.interno.it`. We could not determine whether the old address redirects, runs in parallel, or is deprecated — so use the address the Ministry publishes today, and treat any guide that sends you to ALI as possibly out of date in other respects too.

## What goes in with it

The Ministry lists the language certificate among the documents the electronic application must include, in these words:

> *"certificazione attestante la conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1 del QCER;"*
>
> "certificate attesting knowledge of the Italian language, not below level B1 of the CEFR."

Alongside it, the same list requires an identity document; a birth certificate and a criminal-record certificate from the authorities of the country of origin; the receipt for the payment of €250; and the details of the electronic *marca da bollo* (revenue stamp) for applications filed in Italy. That part of the list reached us as a paraphrase of the Ministry's page rather than a transcription, so we report it rather than reproduce it, and we cannot tell you it is exhaustive. Read it yourself at <https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/cittadinanza-invia-la-tua-domanda>.

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Language certificate at B1 or above | From a body on the Ministry's current list, or an Italian *titolo di studio* obtained in Italian |
| Identity document | — |
| Birth certificate | From the authorities of the country of origin |
| Criminal-record certificate | From the authorities of the country of origin |
| Receipt for the €250 contribution | See the fee note below |
| Electronic *marca da bollo* details | For applications filed in Italy |

Once complete, the application is transmitted electronically to the **Prefettura** for applicants resident in Italy, or to the **Consolato** for applicants resident abroad.

## The €250 contribution — with its date

A MAECI consular notice states the contribution *"è confermato in € 250,00, ai sensi dell'art. 9-bis della legge 5 febbraio 1992, n. 91"*, and that it is owed for all applications filed from **5 October 2018**. That notice is dated **January 2019**, and we cite it as at its date rather than as a guaranteed current figure. The €250 figure is also repeated in the document list on the Ministry's current filing page, which is the better reason to expect it still to hold. Check the amount on the Ministry page before you pay.

## At a consulate: originals and certified copies

If you file from abroad, the consulate handles the verification. The Italian embassy in Berlin describes making a *copia conforme* (certified copy) of the original certificate during that check, with a payment of €10.00 under tariff article 71.

That is one post's published practice, and consular fees and document requirements vary between posts. Do not budget €10 at a different consulate on the strength of it. Check your own post's page, and ask specifically whether they want the original certificate, a certified copy, a translation, or a legalisation — none of which we could verify beyond that one post.

## Plan the calendar backwards

Because the evidence must exist at filing, the exam is the constraint, not the application. Three intervals sit between deciding to sit an exam and having a certificate in hand: the enrolment deadline before the sitting, the wait for results, and the wait for the certificate itself to reach the exam centre. Each awarding body publishes its own figures for these, and they are exam-provider information rather than law — check them on the body's own calendar and results pages, and add slack, because a certificate that arrives after your intended filing date is a certificate that does not help you.

## FAQ

**Does my certificate expire?**
No official source we could reach addresses how long a language certificate stays acceptable for a citizenship application. Siena's FAQ says a CILS certificate *"attualmente"* — currently — has no expiry, and Perugia states that CELI certificates have no expiry. Both are awarding bodies speaking about their own certificates. Neither is the Ministry speaking about your file. If your certificate is several years old, ask the Prefettura before relying on it.

**Can I file first and add the certificate afterwards?**
The statute says the attestation happens at the moment of filing. We have found no official provision for supplying it later, and we do not describe one.

**Do I upload a scan or hand over the original?**
The application is electronic and the certificate is listed among its attachments; consular practice at the post we checked involves presenting the original and taking a certified copy. What your specific office wants is a question for that office.

**How long will the decision take?**
We are not publishing a number. A January 2019 consular notice recorded a term for naturalisation applications, but that period has been the subject of later legislative change and litigation, and we could not verify what is current. Ask the Prefettura or Consolato handling your file.
