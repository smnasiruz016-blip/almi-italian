Both certifications run on the same administrative shape: a university in Italy owns the exam, and a network of affiliated venues around the world administers it under contract. You do not enrol with Siena or with Perugia. You enrol with a centre, and the centre answers to the university.

That shape decides a surprising number of practical things — which sessions you can reach, when your enrolment actually closes, how much you pay, and where your certificate ends up.

## CILS: sedi convenzionate

Unistrasi puts it directly:

> "Gli esami CILS hanno luogo presso le sedi con le quali l'Università per Stranieri di Siena ha stipulato una apposita convenzione."
>
> *CILS exams take place at the venues with which the Università per Stranieri di Siena has entered into a specific agreement.*

Outside Italy, the same page states that the Italian Cultural Institutes abroad and other partners coordinate and organise the exams locally. Unistrasi publishes two directories — one of venues in Italy, one of venues abroad — plus a page for organisations that want to become a centre themselves.

To enrol, the registration page is unambiguous: in Italy and abroad, you contact the sede d'esame where you intend to sit the exam. There is no candidate-facing online booking at the university. A portal exists at iscrizionicils.unistrasi.it, but it is for exam centres, not for candidates — do not spend an afternoon trying to register there.

Useful contacts published by the CILS Centre: infocils@unistrasi.it, gelo@unistrasi.it, nasimi@unistrasi.it, and the number it prints as tel +39 0577 240 100/11.

## CELI: centri d'esame convenzionati

CVCL's instruction is the same in substance: *"Per iscriversi agli esami CELI è necessario rivolgersi ad uno dei Centri d'esame convenzionati"* (*To enrol in CELI exams you must contact one of the affiliated exam centres*). The centre collects the fee at the moment you submit the application, and it is the centre's job to tell you the amount and the payment method.

Two rules give the CELI network its texture. Exams are held simultaneously at all centres worldwide, time zones permitting, on the dates in the published calendar. And each level must be administered in its own room — the Regolamento states that multiple levels cannot be run in the same room, which is one reason a small centre may not offer every level at every session.

CVCL's general contact for candidates is certificazione@unistrapg.it.

## What the centre controls, and what it does not

| | CILS (Siena) | CELI (Perugia) |
|---|---|---|
| Who you enrol with | The sede d'esame | The Centro d'esame |
| Session dates | Set by the university | Set by the university's calendar |
| Enrolment deadline | Published per session by Unistrasi | Set by the centre, but never later than the CVCL cut-off |
| Fee collection | Collected by the venue, at the rate set in the convention | Collected by the centre at enrolment; amount owed to CVCL |
| Marking | Central, at the Centro CILS in Siena | Written part corrected centrally at CVCL |
| Certificate | Collected from the venue where you sat | Issued electronically by CVCL, sent to the centre, distributed by it |

The pattern is consistent: the centre is your counter, not your examiner. Where a decision is about content, scoring or dates, the university has made it. Where it is about a room, a receipt or a deadline that is earlier than the published one, the centre has.

## On the day

For CILS, Unistrasi publishes the running order and the rules. Exams begin with the listening tests. Answers must be written with a black pen only — pencil or coloured ink invalidates the answer. Bring identification and expect to sign the attendance sheet. Orals are usually held in the afternoon, are recorded, and consist of a conversation between the candidate and the administrator plus a monologue.

For CELI, the Regolamento's articles on exam-room conduct cover identification, permitted pens, spacing between candidates and prohibited behaviour, but we were unable to verify the wording of those specific provisions to the standard we hold ourselves to. Treat only the one-level-per-room rule as confirmed here, and ask your centre for its own instructions before the day.

## Choosing between two centres

Nothing official ranks centres, and the exam is the same wherever you sit it — same paper, same central marking. Practical grounds for choosing are therefore mundane and legitimate: which sessions the centre actually runs at your level, how early its enrolment closes, how far you must travel for an oral that may be in the afternoon, and how it handles collection of the certificate afterwards. If you have moved since sitting, ask early: Unistrasi's published instruction is to collect the certificate from the venue where you took the exam, and we found no official statement about posting it elsewhere.

## Frequently asked

**Can I sit CILS or CELI outside Italy?**
Yes, both operate international networks. For CILS, Italian Cultural Institutes abroad and other partners coordinate the exams locally, and Unistrasi publishes a directory of venues abroad. For CELI, centres worldwide run the same calendar simultaneously.

**Does the €100 CILS B1 Cittadinanza fee apply abroad too?**
That is how Unistrasi publishes it — the same figure in Italy and abroad. See our page on costs for what is and is not official about exam pricing.

**Can I sit the written exam at one centre and the oral at another?**
No official source describes such an arrangement. Assume one centre for the whole sitting and ask if your circumstances force the question.

**My centre quoted an enrolment deadline earlier than the published one. Is that allowed?**
For CELI, yes — the regulation lets the centre set its own deadline provided it respects the CVCL cut-off, which makes earlier permissible and later not.
