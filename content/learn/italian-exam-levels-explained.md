A number in an exam name is not a CEFR level. CELI 1 is A2. CELI 2 is B1. CILS UNO is B1 as well, despite the "one". Readers who book by the number rather than by the level lose a session and a fee, and in an immigration timetable a lost session is the expensive part.

Both awarding bodies work from the same scale — the *Quadro Comune Europeo di Riferimento per le Lingue* (QCER), which English-language documents call the CEFR, running A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2. Unistrasi states that CILS covers all six levels of communicative competence identified by that framework. CVCL in Perugia describes its generic CELI exams as covering the CEFR levels from A1 to C2. Beyond that shared scale, the two naming systems are separate and should never be mixed in a sentence.

## The CILS names — Università per Stranieri di Siena

| CILS exam | CEFR level | Grouping used by Unistrasi |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | A1 | Basic |
| A2 | A2 | Basic |
| CILS UNO–B1 | B1 | Autonomous |
| CILS DUE–B2 | B2 | Autonomous |
| CILS TRE–C1 | C1 | Competent |
| CILS QUATTRO–C2 | C2 | Competent |

Unistrasi adds a point that matters if you are budgeting your time: *"Ogni livello CILS è autonomo e completo"* — each CILS level is self-standing and complete. You do not need to pass A2 before sitting a B1 exam.

### Three modules inside CILS UNO–B1

The B1 level is not a single paper. Unistrasi's page for CILS UNO–B1 states that the level contains three modules: **B1 standard, B1 A and B1 C**. The same page states that B1 is the level required for obtaining Italian citizenship, and the CILS FAQ repeats it: B1 is described as the level of autonomy, also required for Italian citizenship.

The module the official calendar prints as **B1 C** is the citizenship one — the exam page is headed for the B1 c tests, while the syllabus and criteria filenames use the descriptive form B1 Cittadinanza. **B1 A is a different module.** No official page we reached defines what B1 A is, so we will not guess, and neither should any site that tells you confidently. If you are booking for citizenship, book the module the calendar lists as B1 C.

## The CELI names — CVCL, Università per Stranieri di Perugia

| CELI exam | CEFR level |
|---|---|
| CELI Impatto | A1 |
| CELI 1 | A2 |
| CELI 2 | B1 |
| CELI 3 | B2 |
| CELI 4 | C1 |
| CELI 5 | C2 |

The descriptor CVCL prints for CELI 2 is the ability *"interagire nell'ambito di situazioni ed argomenti familiari e di loro interesse"* (*to interact within familiar situations and topics of interest to them*) — the practical shape of B1 as Perugia frames it.

### Three families, and the suffix that identifies them

CVCL runs three families of CELI exam:

- **Generici** — general Italian for schooled adults, A1 to C2, printed with no suffix.
- **Adolescenti** — for candidates aged 12 to 18, across three levels from A2 to B2, printed with the suffix **a**: A2 CELI 1 a, B1 CELI 2 a, B2 CELI 3 a.
- **Immigrati** — for adult immigrants, across three levels from A1 to B1, printed with the suffix **i**: A1 CELI Impatto i, A2 CELI 1 i, B1 CELI 2 i cittadinanza.

That last string is the one to memorise if you are applying for citizenship through Perugia. The immigrati page describes the audience as *cittadini stranieri legati a un progetto migratorio stanziale* — foreign citizens tied to a settled migration project — and connects CELI 1 i (A2) to the EU long-term residence permit and CELI 2 i to citizenship.

### One recognition claim worth knowing

CVCL states that MIUR recognises CELI 3 as valid evidence of Italian for university enrolment in Italy, within the quota each university reserves for non-Italophone applicants. CELI certificates also carry the ALTE Q Mark, a European quality seal awarded after audit. Neither statement concerns immigration; do not carry it into a citizenship file.

## Mapping levels onto procedures

Citizenship under arts. 5 and 9 of L. 91/1992 requires Italian at not below B1. The EU long-term residence permit route works at A2. So:

- **Citizenship** → a B1 certificate. In CILS terms that is the UNO–B1 level; in CELI terms, CELI 2.
- **Long-term residence permit** → A2. In CILS terms the A2 level; in CELI terms, CELI 1.

The exam names differ; the legal level does not.

## Frequently asked

**Is a higher level accepted where a lower one is required?**
The legal wording for citizenship is "not below B1", so a higher CEFR level meets a B1 requirement on its face. For the residence-permit route the Ministry's wording is "not below A2". Confirm the certificate you hold is from a body on the relevant official list.

**Do I have to climb the levels one at a time?**
Not for CILS: Unistrasi states each level is autonomous and complete. We found no equivalent published statement for CELI, so if you plan to skip levels there, ask your exam centre.

**What is "B1 A"?**
Officially confirmed to exist as one of the three CILS UNO–B1 modules, and otherwise undocumented on the pages we could reach. Do not book it in the hope that it is the citizenship module.

**Do CELI Adolescenti exams work for immigration?**
They are defined by age, 12 to 18, and cover A2 to B2. We found no official statement about their use in immigration procedures; check the current official page and ask your Prefettura.
