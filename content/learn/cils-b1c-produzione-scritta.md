**Eighty to one hundred and twenty words.** That is the length Siena's criteria document prints beside the written task: "Prova a tema (80 - 120 parole)". Shorter ranges circulate widely — including, until recently, inside our own practice tool, which is why we are stating the official figure this plainly. If a teacher, a video or an app has told you a smaller number, the criteria PDF on Siena's own B1 Cittadinanza page overrides it.

You get **40 minutes** for that one text, and it is worth **12 points**.

## The task itself

The syllabus describes a single writing task: an email either requesting information or resolving a problem with a service. That is a functional, transactional piece of writing with a reader on the other end — someone at an office, a company, a landlord, a service provider. It is not an essay, and there are no marks described anywhere for opinion, originality or length beyond the range.

Forty minutes for around a hundred words is generous by design. The time is there for planning, for writing carefully, and for checking — not for producing volume.

## The five criteria, and their weights

This is the part most candidates never see, and it is published. Siena's criteria document lists five sub-criteria for the written task, each with a maximum:

| Criterion (Italian) | English | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Efficacia comunicativa | Communicative effectiveness - does it do the job it was asked to do | 3 |
| Adeguatezza stilistica | Stylistic appropriateness - register suited to the reader | 1 |
| Correttezza morfosintattica | Morphosyntactic accuracy - grammar and sentence structure | 4 |
| Adeguatezza e ricchezza lessicale | Lexical appropriateness and range | 3 |
| Ortografia e punteggiatura | Spelling and punctuation | 1 |
| **Total** | | **12** |

Read the weights, not just the names. **Correttezza morfosintattica carries 4 points** — "correttezza morfosintattica: fino a punti 4" (up to 4 points), more than any other single criterion. Grammatical accuracy and vocabulary together account for 7 of the 12. Efficacia comunicativa, at 3, rewards actually completing the communicative task set. Register and spelling are worth 1 each: real points, but not the ones to spend your last ten minutes on if the grammar is shaky.

## What that implies for practice

The weights suggest a rather unglamorous priority order, and it is the opposite of what many candidates instinctively do:

- **Answer the prompt completely.** An elegant email that omits one of the things the task asked for gives away efficacia comunicativa.
- **Write sentences you can control.** Four points ride on accuracy. A simple, correct sentence outscores an ambitious, broken one on this scale.
- **Vary the vocabulary within your range.** Three points sit on lexical appropriateness and range, not on rarity.
- **Keep the register consistent.** One point, quickly earned by choosing a form of address and staying with it.
- **Reserve the last minutes for spelling, accents and punctuation.** One point, and the cheapest one on the sheet.

## Questions readers ask

**What happens if I write fewer than 80 or more than 120 words?**
Siena publishes the range but no explicit penalty for missing it, so we will not invent one. Write inside the range; it is the only instruction you have been given.

**Are the criteria for the written task the same as for the spoken one?**
No. The spoken task uses four criteria with different weights, including one for pronunciation. They are set out on the speaking page.

**Is the email marked at my exam centre?**
No. Siena states that all exam papers are marked centrally at the Centro CILS, which also wrote the criteria.

**If my writing fails but everything else passes, do I resit only the writing?**
No. There is no capitalizzazione at this level. The entire exam is taken again in the next session.
