Same level, same university, two different exams. Siena's B1 band — officially **CILS UNO-B1** — contains three modules: B1 standard, B1 A and B1 C. B1 C is the citizenship module described throughout this section; B1 standard is the general certification at the same CEFR level. (B1 A is a separate module again, and no official page we could reach defines it, so we will not guess at it.)

Neither exam is the easy one. They are differently shaped, and the shape is what should drive the choice.

## The two exams side by side

An Italian government information sheet sets out the comparison directly:

| | B1 Cittadinanza | B1 standard |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | 2 tests | 3 tests |
| Reading | 2 tests (reading plus analysis of structures) | 3 reading tests plus 4 on analysis of structures |
| Writing | 1 task | 2 tasks |
| Speaking | 1 task | 2 tasks |
| Duration | about 2h15 | about 3h55 |
| Capitalizzazione | not allowed | allowed |

## The scoring is not the same either

The standard exam's rule is published and unambiguous: five abilità at 20 points each, **{{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points maximum, 55 to pass, with a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} points in each skill**, per Siena's Linee guida. The citizenship module's own criteria document counts differently — four abilità at 12 points each, **48 in total** — and Siena publishes **no pass mark and no per-section minimum** for it.

That silence is why our practice product's {{CILS_B1C_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} floor and {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} total are labelled as ours: we derived them from the standard exam's published 11-out-of-20 rule (55 per cent of 12 is 6.6, rounded to 7; four skills at 7 make 28), because Siena has published nothing for the Cittadinanza module to derive them from.

## The trade-off, stated plainly

The citizenship module is shorter, has fewer tests, and has a published fee of 100 euro in Italy and abroad. It is also **all-or-nothing**: no capitalizzazione, so a failure in any one skill means retaking the entire exam in the next session.

The standard exam is substantially longer and has more tests to prepare for — but if you fail one skill, the government sheet describes it as retakable in the insufficient part only.

So the question is not which exam is easier. It is which failure mode you would rather face: more material and more hours, with a partial retake if it goes wrong; or a shorter sitting where one weak skill costs you everything.

A second consideration, if your reasons extend past the application: Siena describes standard B1 as "il livello minimo per poter svolgere un corso di formazione professionale" (the minimum level for undertaking a vocational training course), and notes that each CILS level is self-standing — "Ogni livello CILS è autonomo e completo" (every CILS level is autonomous and complete).

## What the law asks for, and what it does not

Worth repeating, because it is the frame around this whole choice: art. 9.1 of Legge 91/1992 requires a certification at not below B1 from a recognised certifying body. It does not name a specific branded exam, and it does not require a module with "cittadinanza" in its title. Which certificates the Ministry of the Interior currently accepts is a matter for the Ministry's own published list — check the current official page before you book anything, and confirm with the office handling your application if you have any doubt.

We support the B1 Cittadinanza format in our practice tool because it is the module built for this audience, not because the law prescribes it.

## Questions readers ask

**Is B1 Cittadinanza recognised in the same way as standard B1?**
Both are CILS certifications issued by the Università per Stranieri di Siena at level B1. What the receiving authority accepts is set out on the Ministry's list rather than by us — check the current official page.

**Can I sit the standard exam instead if I prefer the banking rule?**
Both modules exist within CILS UNO-B1 and are offered by the exam centres, though not every session offers every module. Siena's exam calendar shows which levels run in each session.

**Is the standard exam more expensive?**
Only the 100 euro figure for B1 Cittadinanza is published openly by Siena. For any other level, ask your exam centre; we will not quote a figure we could not verify.

**Do the two exams share the same criteria documents?**
No. B1 Cittadinanza has its own criteria PDF with four skills at 12 points; the standard exam works to the Linee guida with five skills at 20.
