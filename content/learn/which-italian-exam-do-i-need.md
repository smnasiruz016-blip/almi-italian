The honest answer is that you do not choose an exam first. You identify the procedure you are filing, read the level that procedure demands, and only then pick a certificate that proves it. Two procedures dominate the questions we receive, and they sit at two different levels.

## Two procedures, two levels

| Procedure | Level required | Where the requirement lives |
|---|---|---|
| Italian citizenship under arts. 5 and 9 of L. 91/1992 (marriage or civil union; naturalisation by residence) | B1 | Art. 9.1 L. 91/1992, inserted in 2018 |
| Permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo (EU long-term residence permit) | A2 | Art. 9 D.Lgs 286/1998 and the DM of 4 June 2010 |

Everything else on this page follows from that split. If you are filing for citizenship, an A2 certificate does nothing for you. If you are applying for the long-term permit, a B1 certificate is above the bar rather than beside it.

## Citizenship: B1, and what the statute actually says

The requirement was created by art. 14, comma 1, lett. a-bis) of D.L. 4 October 2018 n. 113, converted with modifications by Legge 1 December 2018 n. 132, which inserted a new art. 9.1 into Legge 5 February 1992 n. 91. The statute reads:

> "La concessione della cittadinanza italiana ai sensi degli articoli 5 e 9 è subordinata al possesso, da parte dell'interessato, di un'adeguata conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1 del Quadro Comune Europeo di Riferimento per la conoscenza delle lingue (QCER)."
>
> *The grant of Italian citizenship under articles 5 and 9 is conditional on the person concerned possessing an adequate knowledge of the Italian language, not below level B1 of the CEFR.* — art. 9.1 L. 91/1992, as reproduced by the Consolato Generale d'Italia in Lugano

Two consequences matter more than most readers expect.

First, the rule bites on applications filed from 4 December 2018 onward. A Ministry of the Interior document puts it plainly: everyone who filed the application electronically from that date must demonstrate the language requirement or show they are exempt.

Second, **the law names a level and a set of certifying bodies — it does not name a branded exam module.** Nothing in the statutory wording we have seen requires a certificate with the word *cittadinanza* on it. Exam providers do publish citizenship-specific modules, and they are convenient, but do not assume from the law alone that a general B1 certificate from a recognised body is refused, or that it is accepted. If your certificate is not the citizenship module, confirm with your Prefettura before you file.

### Which bodies count

The Ministero dell'Interno *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025 names five certifying universities and bodies — Università per Stranieri di Siena (CILS), Università per Stranieri di Perugia (CELI), Università degli Studi Roma Tre (CERT.IT), Società Dante Alighieri (PLIDA) and Università per stranieri "Dante Alighieri" di Reggio Calabria — plus the network of *Centri provinciali per l'istruzione degli adulti* (C.P.I.A., provincial adult-education centres).

An older Ministry of the Interior / Prefettura document from June 2024 lists only four, omitting Reggio Calabria, and so does the Farnesina's central page on language certifications. We are not going to resolve that for you: the two lists genuinely disagree, and we could not find the instrument that added the fifth body. Check the current official page before you rely on any body outside the four that appear on both lists.

An Italian school or university qualification can also serve. The vademecum accepts a qualification from a public or state-recognised educational institution, from lower-secondary level upward, and adds one condition: *"Il titolo di studio deve essere conseguito necessariamente in lingua italiana"* (*the qualification must necessarily have been obtained in the Italian language*).

## The long-term residence permit: A2

Here the default route is not a commercial exam at all but a state test, governed by the Ministerial Decree of 4 June 2010. The Ministry of the Interior describes it as the test to be taken by a foreign national legally resident in Italy for more than five years who intends to request the EU long-term permit. The application goes to the Prefettura through testitaliano.interno.it; the competent office convenes the candidate within 60 days; a fail can be retaken only after 90 days.

You are not required to sit that test if you already hold an attestation of Italian at not below A2 from Università per Stranieri di Siena, Università per Stranieri di Perugia, Università degli Studi Roma Tre or Società Dante Alighieri — and note that this page lists **four** bodies, not the citizenship vademecum's five. Other listed routes include a CPIA qualification attesting A2, recognition of A2 within the credits of the *accordo di integrazione* (integration agreement), an Italian secondary-school diploma or Italian university studies, and certain admission categories such as senior managers, highly qualified workers, university professors and researchers.

Separately from those, the decree carves two groups out entirely: children under fourteen, and a foreign national with serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language arising from age, illness or disability, certified by a public health facility.

## If you are exempt, you may need no exam at all

For citizenship, the Ministry's filing page lists as exempt those who signed the *accordo di integrazione*; holders of the EU long-term residence permit; and people with serious language-learning limitations arising from age, illness or disability — the third limb reflecting the Constitutional Court's ruling no. 25/2025. Consular pages add a date qualifier to the permit limb that does not appear in the statute or on the Ministry's own pages; that conflict is unresolved, and it is worth a question to your Prefettura or consulate if it affects you.

## Frequently asked

**Does an A2 certificate count towards citizenship later?**
It does not meet the B1 requirement. Whether it helps you toward the EU long-term permit, which is itself an exemption from the citizenship language duty, is a separate question about your permit, not about the certificate.

**Is the under-14 exemption also good for citizenship?**
We cannot tell you that it is. The under-14 carve-out we can verify sits in the A2 residence-permit decree. Whether minors are exempt from the citizenship B1 rule is not something we found stated officially — check the current official page.

**Do I have to take the "cittadinanza" module specifically?**
The law we can read specifies a level and a list of bodies. It does not name modules. Confirm with your Prefettura if you hold or plan to take a general B1 certificate.
