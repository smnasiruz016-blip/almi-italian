The listening section is the first thing that happens. Siena's page on how CILS exams are run states it directly: "Gli esami CILS cominciano con le prove di ascolto" (CILS exams begin with the listening tests). You will not be settling in, warming up on an easy reading text, or finding your rhythm first. The audio starts when the room is ready, and the section is over in about thirty minutes.

## What the section contains

The syllabus for B1 Cittadinanza sets out two tests, roughly half an hour in total, on 400-700 words of recorded material. The task types are multiple choice with three options, and information identification. **Every text is played twice.**

The criteria document gives the arithmetic:

| Test | Format | Items | Points per item | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ascolto, prova 1 | Multiple choice - "Test a scelta multipla composto da 6 item" | 6 | 1 | 6 |
| Ascolto, prova 2 | True/false, as the criteria document labels it | 12 | 0.5 | 6 |
| **Section total** | | **18** | | **12** |

One wording difference is worth knowing about, because it changes what you expect to see on the page. The syllabus describes the second task type as information identification; the criteria document calls it "Test vero/Falso composto da 12 item" (a true/false test made up of 12 items). Both are official Siena documents. Prepare for a statement-by-statement task in which you decide whether each statement matches what you heard, and either description will fit.

## Why the two tests are not equally forgiving

Six items at one point each, and twelve items at half a point each, both come to six. But the shapes are different. In prova 1, one wrong answer costs a full point out of the section's twelve. In prova 2, a wrong answer costs half a point, so the damage from a single misheard detail is smaller, but there are twice as many chances to take it.

Guessing rules are not published anywhere we could verify, so we will not tell you that a wrong answer is penalised or that it is free. What we can say is that leaving an item blank guarantees zero for it.

## Using the second play properly

The audio being played twice is the single most useful structural fact in this section, and it is easy to waste. A workable habit, and this is our editorial suggestion rather than anything Siena publishes: use the first play to answer what you are sure of and to mark the items you are not, and the second play to hunt only for the marked ones. Trying to verify all eighteen items twice is how candidates run out of listening time while the recording moves on.

Answers go on the answer sheet in black pen only. Siena's exam-conduct page says answers written in pencil or in another colour are not valid — a detail that belongs to every section but bites hardest here, because there is no time to rewrite anything during the audio.

## Official material to practise on

Siena publishes free B1 Cittadinanza material on the module's own page: a sample paper (Prototipo Quaderno), the audio file, the transcripts (TRASCRIZIONI) and updated answer keys (Chiavi). Working through the official sample with the official keys tells you more about the real item types than any commercial imitation, ours included.

## Questions readers ask

**Can I take notes while the audio plays?**
Nothing we could verify addresses note-taking in this section. Ask the invigilator at your centre on the day rather than assuming either way.

**How many words of audio is 400-700 in practice?**
It is the total across both tests, not per text. Expect short, functional recordings rather than one long lecture.

**Does a strong listening score cover a weak writing score?**
Not in a way you should rely on. There is no capitalizzazione at this level, and Siena does not publish how the four skills combine into a result, so plan to be adequate in all four rather than excellent in one.

**Is the recording the same in Italy and abroad?**
The exam is centrally set and centrally marked by the Centro CILS in Siena, so the paper is the university's, not the centre's.
