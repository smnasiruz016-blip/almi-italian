One mapping error travels further than anything else written about this exam: the belief that *CELI Impatto* is A2 and *CELI 1* is A1. It is the reverse. If you book on that assumption you can sit an exam a full CEFR level below the one your application needs, discover it only when the certificate arrives, and lose a session. This page exists to get the mapping right.

## The six generic CELI exams, as CVCL prints them

| Exam name | CEFR level | What CVCL's descriptor says the candidate can do |
|---|---|---|
| CELI Impatto | A1 | Contact level: introductory competence built on memorised routines |
| CELI 1 | A2 | Exchange basic information about self, family and work in areas of immediate relevance |
| CELI 2 | B1 | Interact within familiar situations and topics of personal interest |
| CELI 3 | B2 | Interact with relative fluency and spontaneity across a wide range of topics |
| CELI 4 | C1 | Express oneself fluently, spontaneously and variedly in work and academic contexts |
| CELI 5 | C2 | Use the language in any context, including complex topics, always effectively |

The numbering starts at *Impatto* rather than at 1, which is exactly why the off-by-one error happens. Read it as a sequence: Impatto, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 mapping onto A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2.

For B1 — the level Italian law sets for the citizenship application — the exam you want is **CELI 2**. CVCL's descriptor for it is:

> "interagire nell'ambito di situazioni ed argomenti familiari e di loro interesse"
> *interact within familiar situations and topics of interest to them.*
> — CVCL, Esami CELI generici

## The suffix convention: no suffix, "i", or "a"

CELI comes in three families, and the family shows up as a one-letter suffix on the exam name. Miss the suffix and you have the level right but the wrong exam.

| Suffix | Family | Who it is for | CEFR range covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | CELI Generici | Schooled adults, general Italian | A1 to C2 |
| **i** | CELI Immigrati | Adult immigrants | A1 to B1 |
| **a** | CELI Adolescenti | Learners aged 12 to 18 | A2 to B2 |

The official exam calendar confirms this independently, because it prints the exam names in full. The immigrant series appears there as:

> "A1 CELI Impatto i   A2 CELI 1 i   B1 CELI 2 i cittadinanza"
> — CVCL, Calendario esami CELI e DILS-PG

Note the last string. The B1 exam in the immigrant series is calendared with the word *cittadinanza* (citizenship) attached to its name. The adolescent rows on the same calendar read "A2 CELI 1 a", "B1 CELI 2 a" and "B2 CELI 3 a" — same numbering logic, different suffix.

So a full name has three moving parts: the word CELI, the number or "Impatto", and possibly a suffix. "CELI 2" and "CELI 2 i" are both B1 exams and are not the same exam.

### One typographical wrinkle on the calendar

The February 2026 and February 2027 rows print "A2 CELI i" where every other immigrant row prints "A2 CELI 1 i". Almost certainly a typo on the source page, but we have not resolved it and neither has CVCL. If you are booking a February immigrant session at A2, confirm the exam code with your centre rather than reading it off the calendar.

## Why the level name changes what you receive

The naming is not cosmetic. The Regolamento sets out a five-letter result scale — A (*ottimo*), B (*buono*), C (*sufficiente*) as passes, D (*insufficiente*) and E (*gravemente insufficiente*) as fails — but it also states that at A1 and A2 the result is communicated as positive or negative, without grading.

The consequence, following the mapping above: a CELI Impatto or CELI 1 candidate does not receive a letter grade at all. A CELI 2 candidate sits a B1 exam and, under the Regolamento's general rule, falls inside the graded range. There is a complication at CELI 2 i, where the criteria document published for that module prints no letter bands; that is covered on the immigrant-series page and we do not resolve it here.

There is a second structural consequence. The *Competenza Linguistica* component of the written part exists only from CELI 3 upwards, per the Regolamento's own definition of what the written part contains. At CELI 2 and below, the written part is reading, writing and listening.

## Which exams run when

Level names also determine the calendar. Two complete sessions a year — June and November — cover all six generic levels. A third session in March covers the first four levels only, A1 to B2. CELI Adolescenti has one session a year, in May. CELI Immigrati has one or more sessions a year according to the published calendar. So a candidate wanting CELI 4 (C1) or CELI 5 (C2) has two opportunities a year; a candidate wanting CELI 2 (B1) has more.

## Common questions

**I was told CELI 1 is A1. Was I told wrong?**
Yes, if the source meant the CEFR level. CVCL's own page maps CELI 1 to A2 and CELI Impatto to A1.

**Is "CELI B1" a real exam name?**
Not as CVCL prints it. The calendar writes the CEFR level and the exam name together — "B1 CELI 2" — but the exam is called CELI 2. When you write to a centre, give both.

**Does the generic CELI 2 count for citizenship?**
We cannot tell you. Government texts specify a level and a list of certifying bodies, not exam modules, and CVCL nowhere states that only CELI 2 i counts or that CELI 2 generico does. Ask your Prefettura and certificazione@unistrapg.it before booking.

**Which one should an adult immigrant book, "i" or generic?**
That depends on the answer to the previous question, which is not officially settled. The immigrant series is described by CVCL as aimed at foreign citizens with a settled migration project, and its B1 module is the one calendared for citizenship.
