One hundred points in total, fifty-five to pass, and a minimum of eleven points in every single skill. That is the scoring rule the Università per Stranieri di Siena publishes for the standard CILS UNO-B1 in its Linee guida (guidelines) PDF, and it is the only CILS B1 pass rule that exists in black and white on an official document. Everything else on this page is built around it.

## What CILS UNO-B1 actually is

CILS UNO-B1 is Siena's name for its B1-level certification. Siena groups its six levels into three bands, and UNO-B1 sits in the "autonomous" band alongside CILS DUE-B2. The university describes standard B1 as "il livello minimo per poter svolgere un corso di formazione professionale" (the minimum level for taking a vocational training course).

One structural point trips people up more than any other: CILS UNO-B1 is not a single exam. Siena's own level page states that UNO-B1 contains three modules — B1 standard, B1 A and B1 C. This page is about B1 standard only. The module the exam calendar lists as B1 C is the citizenship module, covered in its own section of this site. As for B1 A, no official page we were able to read defines what it is, so we will not guess.

Each level is self-contained. There is no obligation to have passed A2 before sitting UNO-B1, and passing UNO-B1 is not a step towards B2 that expires if you stop there.

## The published pass rule

| Element | Published figure |
|---|---|
| Skills assessed (abilità) | 5 |
| Points per skill | 20 |
| Maximum total | 100 |
| Minimum total to pass | 55 |
| Minimum in each individual skill | 11 |

Source: Unistrasi, Linee guida CILS.

The per-skill floor is the part candidates underestimate. Fifty-five points spread unevenly does not pass if one skill comes in at nine. A strong reading score cannot rescue a weak speaking score below the floor, and the reverse is equally true.

## What the standard exam contains

An Italian government source — the information sheet published by the Istituto Italiano di Cultura in Algiers — sets out the shape of the standard B1 paper:

| Component | Standard CILS UNO-B1 |
|---|---|
| Listening | 3 tests |
| Reading and structures | 3 reading tests plus 4 structure-analysis tests |
| Writing | 2 tasks |
| Speaking | 2 tasks |
| Approximate duration | about 3 hours 55 minutes |
| Capitalizzazione | allowed |

Nearly four hours is a long sitting, and the length is a genuine planning factor rather than a detail. The same source confirms that the standard exam allows capitalizzazione (banking a passed skill so that only the failed part is re-sat), which the citizenship module does not.

## Sittings and fees

Siena runs six sessions a year. Its 2026 calendar names which levels each session carries, and only two of the six — 16 June and 3 December — are listed as covering all levels; the other four name specific modules. Before you commit to a date, open the official calendar for the year in question and confirm that the session you want actually carries standard B1 at your centre.

On price, the university publicly quotes 100 euros for the citizenship module, in Italy and abroad. It publishes no current figure for standard UNO-B1 that we could verify. Siena's own instructions to exam centres say the centre collects the fee set out in its convention with the university, so the price is not something a centre invents — but the amount for this level is not published, and your centre is the place to ask.

Registration itself is always through an exam centre: "Per sostenere un esame CILS, devi rivolgerti, in Italia e all'estero, alla sede d'esame dove hai intenzione di sostenere l'esame" (to sit a CILS exam you must apply to the exam centre where you intend to sit it), as the registration page puts it. There is no candidate-facing central sign-up.

## Questions people actually ask

**Is standard UNO-B1 accepted for citizenship?**
The law requires a certification at not below B1 from a recognised certifying body, and Siena is one of those bodies. The specific question of which certificates a prefettura or consulate will accept is a legal one, and the accepted-certificates page on this site handles it from the Ministry's own list. Do not take a general reassurance from an exam page as a filing decision.

**If I fail one skill, do I re-sit everything?**
No — the standard exam allows capitalizzazione, so you re-sit only the skill you did not pass. How long that credit lasts is genuinely disputed between two official Unistrasi pages, which we set out on the capitalizzazione page rather than pretending it is settled.

**Is 55 out of 100 the same as "55 per cent right"?**
Not necessarily. The marks are points awarded by central markers at the Centro CILS against published criteria, not a raw percentage of correct answers, and productive tasks are scored on weighted criteria.

**Should I sit standard B1 or the shorter citizenship module?**
That depends on why you need the certificate and how you would cope with an all-or-nothing sitting. We compare the two head to head on a dedicated page rather than telling you one is the soft option — neither is.
