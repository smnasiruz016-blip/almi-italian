Italian citizenship law began asking for language evidence with art. 9.1 of Legge 5 febbraio 1992, n. 91, inserted by the decree-law of 4 October 2018 and converted by **Legge 1 dicembre 2018, n. 132** — a number on which official sources disagree, since the June 2024 Prefettura document we cite below gives the conversion law as n. 136 while the Gazzetta Ufficiale and MAECI give n. 132; we cite 132 and do not resolve it. The statute conditions the grant of citizenship on "un'adeguata conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1" (an adequate knowledge of Italian, not below level B1), for applications under articles 5 and 9 of that law. A Ministry of the Interior document states that everyone who filed electronically from 4 December 2018 onward must show that requirement or an exemption.

Note what the law does **not** say: it does not name a particular branded exam. It asks for a qualification or a certificate from a recognised certifying body at not less than B1. The Università per Stranieri di Siena, which issues CILS, built one module specifically for this audience — and that module is what this section of the site is about.

## What CILS B1 Cittadinanza is

CILS (Certificazione di Italiano come Lingua Straniera) is described by Siena as "un titolo di studio rilasciato dall'Università per Stranieri di Siena" (a qualification issued by the University for Foreigners of Siena). It covers all six CEFR levels, and the B1 band is officially called **CILS UNO-B1**. That band contains three modules: B1 standard, B1 A and B1 C.

**B1 C is the citizenship module.** Siena's own calendar and page headings use the labels "B1 c" and "B1 modulo Cittadinanza"; "CILS B1 Cittadinanza" is the descriptive name used in the syllabus and criteria filenames. One naming trap is worth spelling out: **"B1 A" on the calendar is a different module and is not the citizenship one.** No official page we could reach spells out the equation "B1 C = B1 Cittadinanza" in a single sentence, so we describe it as the module the calendar lists as B1 C.

The syllabus states the target audience plainly — adults living in Italy or abroad interested in obtaining Italian citizenship — and cites Legge 1 dicembre 2018, n. 132 as the reason the module exists.

## The four sections

The syllabus sets out four abilità (skills), tested in one sitting:

| Section | Tests | Time | What it contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ascolto (listening) | 2 | about 30 minutes | 400-700 words of audio in total; multiple choice with three options, plus information identification. Texts are played twice |
| Comprensione della lettura e Riflessione grammaticale (reading and grammar) | 2 | 40 minutes | 350-500 words; information identification, plus a cloze |
| Produzione scritta (writing) | 1 | 40 minutes | An email asking for information or resolving a problem with a service; {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}-{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} words |
| Produzione orale (speaking) | 1 | about 10 minutes | A personal presentation of about a minute, then a dialogue of 2-3 minutes |

Total time is "circa 2h 15'" (about two hours fifteen) according to an Italian government information sheet published by the Istituto Italiano di Cultura in Algiers. Siena's syllabus gives only the per-section timings.

Siena describes the exam's design as aligned to the CEFR: "L'esame CILS B1 c ha un costrutto allineato al livello B1 del Quadro Comune Europeo di Riferimento per le Lingue" (the CILS B1 c exam has a construct aligned to CEFR level B1).

## The rule that changes how you prepare

For this module there is no capitalizzazione (banking a passed skill for a later sitting). If you fail one skill, the whole exam is taken again in the next session. That single rule is the reason a citizenship candidate cannot treat any section as the one to sacrifice, and it has its own page in this section.

## Fee, sessions and where you sit it

The exam fee published by Siena is **100 euro, in Italy and abroad**. That is the only CILS figure we will quote, because it is the only one the university publishes openly; for any other level, ask your exam centre. Exams take place at centres that have signed a convention with Siena, and abroad the Italian Cultural Institutes and other referenti organise them locally.

There are six sessions a year and B1 C is offered in all six. In 2026 the sittings are 19 February, 14 April, 16 June, 22 July, 21 October and 3 December; in 2027, 18 February, 8 April, 10 June, 22 July, 21 October and 2 December. Each has its own registration deadline — for 19 February 2026, Siena writes "iscriviti entro il 19 gennaio" (register by 19 January). Check the deadline for your session before you plan anything around it.

## Questions readers ask

**Is CILS B1 Cittadinanza required by law?**
No. The law requires B1 evidence from a recognised body. This module is one way to produce it. Which certificates the Ministry of the Interior currently accepts is a question for the Ministry's own published list — check the current official page before you commit.

**How long does the whole thing take on the day?**
About two hours fifteen for the written sections; the speaking test is short and often held in the afternoon.

**Do I have to take all four sections in the same session?**
Yes. There is no banking at this level.

**Are there official practice materials?**
Yes, and they are free on Siena's B1 Cittadinanza page: the syllabus, a sample paper (Prototipo Quaderno), transcripts, answer keys, the criteria document and an audio file.
