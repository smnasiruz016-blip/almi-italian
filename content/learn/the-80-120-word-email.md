Eighty to a hundred and twenty words. Forty minutes. One email. That is the whole of the CILS B1 Cittadinanza *produzione scritta*, and the range is printed in Siena's own assessment criteria as *"Prova a tema (80 - 120 parole)"* — a themed task of {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}} to {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} words, worth a maximum of 12 points.

We are opening with the number because getting it wrong is expensive in a specific, avoidable way. A candidate who has spent three months writing 60-word answers will write a 60-word answer on exam day, under pressure, in a room where nobody will tell them it is short. Shorter targets circulate widely — including, we should say plainly, in an earlier version of our own practice material, which is why this page exists.

## What the task is

Per the B1 Cittadinanza syllabus, the written test is a single task: an email that either requests information or sorts out a problem with a service. It is one of four tests in an exam lasting roughly two hours and fifteen minutes in total, and you have 40 minutes for this one.

That framing matters more than it looks. An email that requests information has to *ask*, clearly and in the right register. An email that resolves a problem has to say what went wrong, what you want done, and by when. Both are two-part or three-part errands. It is hard to run a three-part errand in sixty words and still sound like a person writing to an office.

## What the 12 points are for

| Criterion (official, max 12) | Points | What it asks of an {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}–{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} word email |
|---|---|---|
| Efficacia comunicativa | 3 | Did you do everything the prompt asked, and would the recipient know what to do next? |
| Adeguatezza stilistica | 1 | Right register: a greeting, a sign-off, formal address where the situation calls for it. |
| Correttezza morfosintattica | 4 | Grammar and sentence construction — the single heaviest band. |
| Adeguatezza e ricchezza lessicale | 3 | Range and precision of vocabulary, not just correctness. |
| Ortografia e punteggiatura | 1 | Spelling, accents, punctuation. |

Two of these are effectively rationed by length. You cannot show lexical range in sixty words, and you cannot fully discharge a two-part errand in sixty words. Siena does not publish descriptors telling us what a marker deducts for an under-length text, so we will not invent one — but you do not need a deduction rule to see that a short answer has fewer opportunities to earn the 3 points for *efficacia comunicativa* and the 3 for *ricchezza lessicale*.

The reverse trap is real too. There is an upper bound of 120, and padding a short message with filler sentences to reach it puts more grammar on the page for the 4-point *correttezza morfosintattica* band to catch.

## Practical length discipline

- **Find out what 100 words looks like in your handwriting.** Write ten practice emails, count each one, and note the number of lines. After that you can check length by counting lines, in seconds, instead of counting words.
- **Aim for about 100.** It sits comfortably inside the range, leaving room for a correction pass without falling out of the bottom.
- **Split the 40 minutes deliberately.** Something like five minutes to work out what the prompt is actually asking and jot the points you must cover, twenty-five to write, ten to reread. The rereading is where the two cheap points live: *adeguatezza stilistica* and *ortografia e punteggiatura* are worth one point each, and a missing accent or a missing sign-off is a much easier fix than a verb tense you never learned.
- **Write in the pen the exam requires.** Unistrasi's exam-conduct page states that answers must be given only with a black pen; pencil or coloured ink invalidates them. Practise with the same pen you will use.

## Our thresholds are ours, not Siena's

We derived those from the official standard CILS UNO–B1 rule — {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points, 55 to pass, minimum {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} per skill, which is 55 per cent, or 6.6 out of {{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}}, rounded up to 7, and 4 × 7 = 28. Siena publishes no pass mark and no per-skill minimum for the Cittadinanza module at all. Treat our 7 as a practice target we can defend the arithmetic of, not as an official bar.

## If you are sitting CELI instead, the counts are different

CELI is Perugia's exam, with its own tasks and its own published word counts. Do not carry the {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}–{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} figure into CELI preparation, and do not carry CELI's figures into CILS.

| Exam and task | Word count |
|---|---|
| CILS B1 Cittadinanza — produzione scritta | {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}–{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} |
| CELI 2 i (B1) Cittadinanza — e-mail formale | minimum 40, maximum 50 |
| CELI 2 i (B1) Cittadinanza — messaggio informale | minimum 80, maximum 90 |
| CELI 2 (B1) generico — task B.3, short letter or e-mail | 90–100 (see note) |

Note on the last row: the first three counts are transcribed from the awarding bodies' own published wording. The 90–100 for CELI 2 generico task B.3 is not — our source records the task itself verbatim but returned the word range as extractor text rather than as printed wording, so confirm it on the CVCL exam-description PDF before you prepare to it.

Note also that CELI 2 i asks for *two* written texts of different registers plus a form-filling task, inside a paper called *Produzione ed Interazione scritta*, whereas CILS B1 Cittadinanza asks for one email. They are not the same preparation.

## Questions worth answering honestly

**Are the words counted exactly? What happens at 79 or 121?**
We do not know, and no official document we have seen says. Siena publishes the range and the criteria; it does not publish a penalty table. Aim for the middle of the range and the question stops mattering.

**Does the email have to be formal?**
The syllabus describes requesting information or resolving a problem with a service, which is normally a formal or semi-formal situation, and *adeguatezza stilistica* is a scored criterion. Read the prompt for who you are writing to, and match it.

**Can I bank a good written mark and resit only the parts I failed?**
Not at this level. B1 Cittadinanza does not allow *capitalizzazione* (banking a passed skill): if one skill is not passed, the whole exam is taken again at the next session. The standard CILS UNO–B1 exam does allow it — that is one of the real trade-offs between the two.
