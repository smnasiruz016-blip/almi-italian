The awarding body matters here, so start with it. CELI — *Certificato di Conoscenza della Lingua Italiana* (certificate of knowledge of the Italian language) — is issued by the CVCL, the *Centro per la valutazione e le certificazioni linguistiche* (centre for language assessment and certification) at the Università per Stranieri di Perugia. That is a different university, a different regulation and a different marking system from CILS, which belongs to the Università per Stranieri di Siena. Nothing on this page transfers to CILS, and no CILS number belongs in a CELI sentence.

## Three families, not one exam

CVCL does not publish a single CELI exam. Its own page describes three separate families, each aimed at a different population.

| Family | Who it is for, per CVCL | CEFR range |
|---|---|---|
| CELI Generici | general Italian for schooled adults | A1 to C2 |
| CELI Adolescenti | learners aged 12 to 18 | A2 to B2 |
| CELI Immigrati | adult immigrants | A1 to B1 |

The immigrant series is the one most readers of this site will meet, because its B1 exam is the module the official calendar labels for citizenship. The naming inside these families is the single most misreported thing about CELI, and it has a page of its own.

## How a CELI exam is built

Every CELI, at every level, is two separately passable units: the *parte scritta* (written part) and the *parte orale* (oral part). The written part is not one skill — it contains reading, writing and listening together, and from CELI 3 upwards a *Competenza Linguistica* (linguistic competence) component as well. The regulation is explicit that both units must be reached:

> "Ogni esame CELI è considerato superato se il candidato ha raggiunto, tanto nella parte scritta quanto in quella orale, il punteggio minimo stabilito…"
> *Each CELI exam is considered passed if the candidate has reached, in both the written part and the oral part, the established minimum score.*
> — Regolamento degli esami CELI, art. 1.10

That architecture drives everything else. Pass thresholds are stated per part, and *capitalizzazione* (banking a passed part) operates on those two parts rather than on individual skills.

The oral is a face-to-face interview between one candidate and a commission of two examiners. The regulation states that the commission assesses the performance on the spot without telling the candidate the score obtained, so do not expect to leave the room knowing how you did.

## How results are expressed

Scores from the individual tests are summed and converted to letters. Three of the five are passes: A (*ottimo*, excellent), B (*buono*, good), C (*sufficiente*, sufficient). D (*insufficiente*) and E (*gravemente insufficiente*) are fails. At A1 and A2 there is no letter grade at all — the regulation says the result is communicated as positive or negative, without grading.

CVCL publishes results approximately 90 working days from the date it receives candidates' scripts. Note where that clock starts: at CVCL's receipt of the papers, not on exam day. Candidates check online with their *numero di matricola* (candidate registration number) and date of birth.

## Enrolment, fees and where you sit

You do not enrol with Perugia directly. CVCL's enrolment page states that candidates must apply through one of the affiliated exam centres, the *Centri d'esame convenzionati*. The centre also collects the fee at the moment the application is submitted, and the regulation requires it to quote the exam fee separately from any preparation course or administrative charge.

CVCL publishes no euro amount for CELI anywhere we could find. That absence is itself the finding: the amount is fixed by the convention between CVCL and each venue, and the regulation makes it the centre's responsibility to tell candidates the figure and the payment method. Ask your centre, and ask for the exam fee alone. The only official percentage in the whole corpus is the re-sit rate under *capitalizzazione*: 70 per cent of the full fee.

## Recognition claims CVCL makes

Two claims sit on CVCL's own pages and can be repeated with attribution. First, that CELI certificates carry the European Q Mark issued by ALTE, obtained after passing audit procedures. Second, that the certificates do not expire:

> "i Certificati CELI non hanno scadenza"
> *CELI certificates have no expiry.*
> — CVCL, Esami CELI

Worth knowing: that statement lives on the CVCL website, not in the Regolamento, which contains no article on validity at all.

CVCL also states that MIUR recognises CELI 3 as evidence of Italian for university enrolment in Italy, within the quota each university reserves for non-Italophone applicants.

For citizenship the Italian government's texts work differently. They name a *level* — B1 — and a list of *enti certificatori* (certifying bodies), on which the Università per Stranieri di Perugia appears. They do not enumerate acceptable exam modules. Treat the Ministry's current list as the only authority, and check it before you file.

## Questions people actually ask

**Is CELI harder or easier than CILS?**
No official source on either side says anything comparative. We do not answer this question, because there is no official material to answer it with.

**Can I appeal a CELI result?**
The regulation provides for *richieste di chiarimenti* (requests for clarification), addressed to certificazione@unistrapg.it, and for the statutory right of access to records under Law 241/1990. That is not a re-mark procedure, and no re-mark procedure is published.

**Does my certificate run out?**
CVCL says CELI certificates have no expiry. Keep that separate from a capitalised partial result, which the regulation limits to one year.

**How much will it cost me?**
Officially unknown. Ask the centre where you intend to sit, and ask them to break the exam fee out from any course fee.
