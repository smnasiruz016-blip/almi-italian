No official document compares CELI and CILS scoring. Not CVCL in Perugia, not Unistrasi in Siena, not the Ministry of the Interior, not MAECI. We looked in the obvious places — the CILS manual for exam venues, the CILS B1 Cittadinanza criteria, CVCL's own pages — and CELI is not mentioned in Siena's documents, nor CILS in Perugia's. The absence is the finding, and it is the reason this page is a pair of columns rather than a verdict.

There is exactly one comparative statement any official source makes, and it comes from the government rather than from either university: both bodies appear on the list of *enti certificatori* whose certificates satisfy the same B1 requirement. The Prefettura's page names the Università per stranieri di Perugia and the Università per stranieri di Siena among them. Officially, the two are treated as alternative routes to one legal threshold, and nothing official ranks them.

## The two systems, kept apart

Read this table column by column. No figure in one column has any application to the other.

| | **CELI 2 (B1)** — CVCL, Università per Stranieri di Perugia | **CILS B1 Cittadinanza** — Università per Stranieri di Siena |
|---|---|---|
| What is scored | Two separately passable parts: *parte scritta* (reading, writing and listening together) and *parte orale* | Four *abilità*: Ascolto; Comprensione della lettura e riflessione grammaticale; Produzione scritta; Produzione orale |
| Points available | Written {{CELI_DUE_WRITTEN_MAX}} + oral {{CELI_DUE_ORAL_MAX}} = **160** | 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 = **48** in Siena's criteria document |
| Thresholds published | Written ≥ 72 · oral ≥ 22 · overall 94 to 160 | **No pass mark stated** in the criteria document |
| How the result is expressed | Five letter grades, A to E, with A to C as passes; no grading at A1 or A2 | Not established from the official documents retrieved |
| Banking a passed part | *Capitalizzazione* of one of the two parts for one year, at 70 per cent of the fee, same level and exam type | *Capitalizzazione* of individual abilità, tracked by matricola — but **not permitted at all** for the B1 Cittadinanza module |
| Results timing | Approximately 90 working days from CVCL's receipt of the scripts | 45 days to 4 months in the centre manual; approximately 3 months on the candidate-facing page |
| Published fee | None published by CVCL; the centre states the amount | €100 in Italy and abroad, quoted by Unistrasi for B1 Cittadinanza |

Two glosses on the CELI column. On result expression, the Regolamento states:

> "Tre sono i gradi che indicano il superamento dell'esame e il conseguimento del certificato: A (ottimo) – B (buono) – C (sufficiente)."
> *Three grades indicate passing the exam and obtaining the certificate: A (excellent) – B (good) – C (sufficient).*
> — Regolamento degli esami CELI, art. 4.2

And on the CILS column, the flagship fact for a citizenship candidate is a prohibition rather than a number:

> "Per questo livello non è consentita la 'capitalizzazione'."
> *For this level, 'capitalisation' is not permitted.*
> — Unistrasi, CILS B1 cittadinanza

## Each system has an internal contradiction. Both stay unresolved here.

### Inside CELI: 94 and 92

The generic CELI 2 criteria document sets the overall minimum at {{CELI_DUE_PASS_FLOOR}} out of {{CELI_DUE_TOTAL_MAX}}. The CELI 2 i cittadinanza criteria document sets it at 92. Both are B1 exams, both total 160, both require 72 in the written part and 22 in the oral. The likeliest reading is that these are two distinct exams with separately set aggregate cut-offs — but no official text says so. Use the number belonging to the exam you booked.

A related gap: the CELI 2 i document prints no A/B/C/D/E bands at all, though the Regolamento's general rule reserves ungraded results for A1 and A2. We confirmed that absence twice and cannot tell you whether a CELI 2 i pass carries a letter.

### Inside CILS: 48 and 100

Unistrasi's own criteria document for B1 Cittadinanza totals {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} points, four skills at 12 each. An Italian government source — the information sheet published by the Italian Cultural Institute in Algiers — states for both B1 exams that the overall maximum is {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points with a minimum of 55, each skill out of {{CILS_STANDARD_SECTION_MAX}} with a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}}. Unistrasi's general assessment page describes B1 out of 100 and does not mention the Cittadinanza module.

One plausible reading is that raw marks out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} are rescaled onto a 100-point reporting scale. No official document says so. Reported, not resolved.

### And on results timing, CILS says two things

Unistrasi's candidate-facing results page says results are available after about three months. Its manual for exam venues gives a window of 45 days — for the B1 Cittadinanza module only — up to four months, depending on level and candidate numbers. Both are official. Neither is a guarantee, and we do not repeat "45 days" as a flat promise.

## A disclosure about our own numbers

AlmiItalian's practice tool shows a working threshold for the **CILS B1 Cittadinanza** module: {{CILS_B1C_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} per skill and at least {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} overall. Those figures are ours. We derived them from Siena's published rule for the standard CILS UNO–B1 exam, where {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_STANDARD_SECTION_MAX}} is the per-skill minimum, scaled to a 12-point skill. They are not published by Unistrasi, they are not official, and they have **no application to CELI whatsoever**. CELI publishes its own thresholds, in its own units, and those are the ones on the left-hand column above.

## What we will not tell you

Which exam is easier. No official source on either side says anything comparative, and this research confirmed there is no material that could be cited for such a claim even if we wanted to make one. Anyone who tells you one is a softer route is telling you something no document supports.

## Questions

**If both satisfy the same legal requirement, does the choice matter?**
It changes what you sit and how failure works, not what the law asks for. The law asks for B1 from a recognised body.

**Can I compare {{CELI_DUE_PASS_FLOOR}} out of {{CELI_DUE_TOTAL_MAX}} with a CILS score?**
No. Different scales, different units, different institutions. There is no published conversion between them, and inventing one would be fiction.

**Which has the better re-sit rules?**
Different rules, not better ones. CELI lets you bank one of two aggregate parts for a year at 70 per cent of the fee; CILS B1 Cittadinanza does not allow banking at all. Whether that suits you depends on how you fail, which nobody knows in advance.

**Where should I check before deciding?**
The Ministry of the Interior's current list of certifying bodies, and then each university's own criteria documents. Not a comparison table — including this one — as a substitute for either.
