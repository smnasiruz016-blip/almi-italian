Nobody sells you a seat at the A2 state test. It is not a CILS sitting, not a CELI sitting, and not something a language school books on your behalf. It is a state procedure run through the **Prefettura**, and the route in is a government website. That single fact matters more than anything else on this page, because the test itself is the alternative to buying a certificate — and because a school that offers to "register you for the state test" is offering something we can find no official basis for.

## What the test is for

The test is governed by the **Decreto del Ministro dell'Interno 4 giugno 2010**, the implementing decree for art. 9 of Legislative Decree 286/1998. Passing it is a condition of issue for the **permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo** (EU long-term residence permit). It is not the citizenship route: citizenship runs on B1 and on art. 9.1 of Law 91/1992, and there is no equivalent state language test for it in anything we could verify.

## The procedure, as the Ministry describes it

The Ministero dell'Interno's page on the *test di conoscenza della lingua italiana* sets out the sequence. We give it here as the Ministry describes it, not as a quotation, because the passage came back to us reworded rather than transcribed.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Apply | The applicant applies to the Prefettura through the site `https://testitaliano.interno.it` |
| 2. Convocation | The competent office convenes the applicant within 60 days, indicating the date and the venue |
| 3. Sit the test | At the venue and on the date indicated |
| 4. Pass | The Questura issues the permit |
| 5. Fail | The test may be retaken only after 90 days |

Two of those numbers are the ones to plan around. **Sixty days** is the window in which you should expect to be called, so a permit application timed tightly against another deadline needs slack. **Ninety days** is the wait after a failure — long enough that a resit can push a permit application into the following season.

## The no-show rule

A separate 90-day clock applies if you simply do not turn up. A Prefettura notice records the change, citing *Circolare n. 716 del 3 febbraio 2014* of the Ministero dell'Interno, effective 11 February 2014:

> *"in caso di assenza ingiustificata alla sessione di test, l'interessato non potrà richiedere una nuova prenotazione prima che siano trascorsi 90 giorni"*
>
> "in the case of unjustified absence from the test session, the person concerned may not request a new booking before 90 days have passed."

A caveat on this one. We read it on a page hosted by a Region (Prefettura di Aosta, on regione.vda.it), not on an `interno.gov.it` domain, and we were not able to locate the circolare itself. Treat it as second-tier until you have seen it on a Ministry page or been told it by your own Prefettura.

## What we cannot tell you about the paper

This is where the honest half of the page starts, and it is a longer list than we would like.

- **The format of the test.** How many sections, how long, what task types, what the pass standard looks like in practice: art. 2 and the following articles of the 2010 decree are where those rules live, and the Gazzetta Ufficiale's article-level pages returned HTTP 403 to us on every attempt. We describe none of it.
- **The A2 threshold's article number.** A2 is confirmed by the Ministry's own page. Which article of the decree fixes it is unverified.
- **Any fee.** No official source we read states a cost for the state test. We neither say it is free nor quote a price.
- **How many attempts are permitted.** The only interval we have is the 90 days after a failure. Whether attempts are capped is not stated on anything we read.
- **Whether the test is required on renewal** of the permit, as opposed to first issue. The decree speaks of *"il rilascio"* — the issuing. Not addressed.
- **Whether the booking site behaves as described today.** We verified the address as the Ministry publishes it. We did not load it.

If you need any of these, ask your Prefettura directly and ask for it in writing. A caseworker's answer about their own procedure is worth more than any third-party guide, ours included.

## The alternative: a certificate instead

Sitting the state test is not the only way. The Ministry's page lists categories who are **non tenuti allo svolgimento del test** — not required to sit it. We give those categories as the Ministry describes them, not as a quotation, because that passage came back to us reworded rather than transcribed, and you should read the list on the source before you skip the test: <https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/test-di-conoscenza-della-lingua-italiana>. The largest of them is holding a certificate at A2 or above from one of the four bodies its A2 page names: Siena, Perugia, Roma Tre, or the Società Dante Alighieri. Others include a CPIA qualification attesting A2, recognition at A2 within the *accordo di integrazione* credits, Italian secondary or university qualifications, and certain admitted professional categories.

Separately again, two groups are carved out of the decree altogether: children under fourteen, and people with serious limitations on the capacity to learn a language certified by a public health facility. Those sit on the exemptions page, because they are exemptions from the requirement rather than substitutes for the test.

Which of the two routes is right for you turns on your own timing and circumstances: a certificate has its own sitting dates and results wait; the state test has a 60-day convocation window and, if it goes wrong, a further 90 days. We do not set the two against each other on price, because no official source we read states a cost for the state test.

## FAQ

**Can I sit the state test if I already have a certificate?**
The Ministry frames certificate holders as not required to sit it, not as barred from it. We have no official statement either way about someone who wants to sit it anyway. Ask the Prefettura.

**Does passing the A2 test help with citizenship?**
Not directly. Citizenship requires B1 evidence under art. 9.1 of Law 91/1992. But the EU long-term permit that the A2 test unlocks is itself one of the categories relieved of the citizenship language obligation — which is a real, if indirect, connection worth understanding.

**Is the state test the same thing as CILS A2 or CELI 1?**
No. Those are exams from Siena and Perugia respectively, sat at exam centres under those universities' own rules and calendars. The state test is a Prefettura procedure under the 2010 decree. Do not mix the two vocabularies when you talk to an office.
