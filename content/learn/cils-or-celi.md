The question people actually ask is "which one is easier". We will not answer it, and the reason is not diplomacy. We searched both awarding bodies' official material and the Italian government's, and **no official source compares CILS and CELI at all** — not on difficulty, not on marking severity, not on anything. Perugia's site compares CELI to other European certificates through ALTE; Siena's exam-venue manual never mentions CELI; the Ministry of the Interior names certifying bodies without ranking them. Anyone who tells you one is softer is telling you something no institution has published.

What the government does say is that both bodies' certificates satisfy the same legal requirement. The Prefettura's page states the standard as *"conoscenza della lingua italiana, non inferiore al livello B1 del quadro comune europeo"* (*knowledge of Italian not lower than level B1 of the Common European Framework*) and lists Università per stranieri di Perugia and Università per stranieri di Siena among the certifying bodies. For that purpose they are alternatives, not a hierarchy.

So choose on mechanics, calendar and geography. Below, each column is sourced only to its own institution.

## CILS — Università per Stranieri di Siena

CILS is a *titolo di studio* (qualification) issued by Unistrasi. Its citizenship-track module is the one the official calendar lists as **B1 C**, and the syllabus describes it as aimed at adults, living in Italy or abroad, interested in obtaining Italian citizenship, under Legge 1 dicembre 2018, n. 132.

- **Shape:** four abilità — Ascolto (2 tests, about 30 minutes), Comprensione della lettura e riflessione grammaticale (2 tests, 40 minutes), Produzione scritta (one {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}–{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} word task, 40 minutes), Produzione orale (about 10 minutes).
- **Length:** about 2h15 in total, per an Italian government information sheet.
- **Marking:** central, at the Centro CILS in Siena.
- **Re-sits:** none of the module can be banked. Unistrasi's page says: *"Per questo livello non è consentita la 'capitalizzazione'. Se l'esame non è superato in qualche abilità, questa non si potrà recuperare, ma si dovrà sostenere di nuovo tutto l'esame nella sessione successiva."* (*For this level 'capitalisation' is not permitted. If the exam is not passed in some skill, that skill cannot be recovered; the whole exam must be taken again at the following session.*)
- **Sessions:** six a year, with B1 C offered at every one.
- **Fee:** €100, quoted by the university for Italy and abroad.
- **Results:** the candidate-facing page says about three months; the manual for exam venues describes a window running from 45 days — for the B1 Cittadinanza module only — up to four months. Those two official statements do not agree, and we report both rather than picking one.

Unistrasi's standard CILS UNO–B1, a different and longer exam, does allow capitalizzazione. That trade-off is covered on its own page.

## CELI — CVCL, Università per Stranieri di Perugia

CELI is issued by the Centro per la valutazione e le certificazioni linguistiche in Perugia. Its citizenship-track exam appears on the official calendar as **B1 CELI 2 i cittadinanza**, and its description PDF is titled "CELI 2 i (B1) - Cittadinanza".

- **Shape:** four prove — Comprensione della Lettura, Produzione ed Interazione scritta (a formal e-mail of 40–50 words and an informal message of 80–90 words), Comprensione dell'Ascolto (20 minutes), and an oral of 10–12 minutes built from five components.
- **Length:** the description PDF gives a total duration of 2 hours and 30 minutes.
- **Architecture:** the regulation treats a CELI as two passable units — the *parte scritta* (which contains reading, writing and listening) and the *parte orale*. Both must reach their minimum.
- **Marking:** the written part is corrected centrally at CVCL by trained examiners; the oral commission is instructed not to tell the candidate the score.
- **Re-sits:** the regulation's art. 1.10 provides capitalizzazione of one part for one year, at 70% of the full fee, at the same level and exam type. We could find no official statement confirming that this mechanism operates for the *immigrati* / cittadinanza modules, so do not plan around it — ask certificazione@unistrapg.it or your centre.
- **Sessions:** two complete sessions a year (June and November), a March session for the first four levels, one May session for CELI Adolescenti, and one or more sessions a year for CELI Immigrati per the published calendar.
- **Fee:** no official euro figure is published. The centre collects it and must tell you the amount.
- **Results:** approximately 90 working days from the date CVCL receives the scripts — note that the clock starts on receipt, not on exam day.

Read each column against its own exam only. The 48 and the 160 are not two scales for one thing, and carrying a number across the gap would be a mistake with consequences.

On the CILS total, two official sources disagree and we do not resolve them. Unistrasi's own criteria document for B1 Cittadinanza totals **48** points, four skills at 12 each. An Italian government source — the information sheet published by the Istituto Italiano di Cultura in Algiers — states for both B1 exams that the overall maximum is **100** points with a minimum of **55**, each skill out of {{CILS_STANDARD_SECTION_MAX}} with a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}}. One plausible reading is that raw marks out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} are rescaled onto a 100-point reporting scale, but no official document says so. Reported, not resolved.

## Frequently asked

**Which is accepted for the citizenship application?**
The Ministry names bodies, not modules. Both Perugia and Siena appear on its lists. Whether a general B1 module rather than a citizenship module is accepted is not something we could verify — check the current official page and ask your Prefettura.

**If I fail one skill, which exam is kinder?**
On the sourced facts: CILS B1 Cittadinanza requires the whole exam again. For CELI, the regulation's one-year, 70%-fee capitalizzazione is written for CELI exams generally, and no source confirms it covers the cittadinanza module. That is an asymmetry in what is published, not a verdict.

**Can I hold a partial CELI result and switch to CILS?**
No official source contemplates it, and CELI's own wording ties a capitalised result to the same level and exam type. Treat the two systems as closed to each other.

**Which gives results faster?**
Both timelines are published as approximations from different starting points, and one of Siena's is internally contradicted. Book the sitting that gives you the largest margin before your filing date rather than betting on a turnaround.
