*"non inferiore al livello B1 del Quadro comune europeo di riferimento per la conoscenza delle lingue – QCER"* — "not below level B1 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages". That phrase, quoted here from the Ministero dell'Interno *Vademecum* of 23 April 2025, is the whole of what the state asks for. It names a level and, elsewhere, a set of recognised certifying bodies. It does not name an exam, a module, or a paper.

Which means the question "what is B1?" has two answers: a legal one, which is short, and a practical one, which is whatever the awarding body you choose puts in front of you. Below is the practical one, taken from the awarding bodies' own published documents and kept in separate columns — because CILS and CELI are two exams from two universities with two scoring systems, and mixing a number from one into a sentence about the other is how misinformation about these exams starts.

## Siena: the CILS citizenship module

Università per Stranieri di Siena publishes the module its calendar lists as **B1 c**. Its syllabus and criteria documents give this shape:

| Section | Tests | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ascolto (listening) | 2 | about 30 min | 400–700 words of audio in total; multiple choice with 3 options, plus information identification; texts played twice |
| Comprensione della lettura e riflessione grammaticale (reading and grammar) | 2 | 40 min | 350–500 words; information identification plus a cloze task |
| Produzione scritta (writing) | 1 | 40 min | An email asking for information or resolving a problem with a service, {{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MIN_WORDS}}–{{CILS_B1C_SCRITTA_MAX_WORDS}} words |
| Produzione orale (speaking) | 1 | about 10 min | A personal presentation of roughly a minute, then a dialogue of 2–3 minutes |

An Italian government source, an Istituto Italiano di Cultura information sheet, gives the total sitting as "circa 2h 15'". The published exam fee for this module is €100 in Italy and abroad.

The single most consequential structural fact sits on Siena's own page for the module:

> *"Per questo livello non è consentita la 'capitalizzazione'. Se l'esame non è superato in qualche abilità, questa non si potrà recuperare, ma si dovrà sostenere di nuovo tutto l'esame nella sessione successiva."*
> "For this level, 'capitalisation' is not allowed. If the exam is not passed in some skill, that skill cannot be recovered; the whole exam must be taken again in the following session."

**Capitalizzazione** — banking a passed section so you only re-sit what you failed — is available on the standard CILS UNO–B1, and not on this module. One weak section takes the whole sitting with it.

### The scoring problem, reported and not resolved

Siena's criteria document for the citizenship module allots a maximum of 12 points to each of the four abilità: **{{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} points in total**. It publishes the sub-criteria too — for writing, efficacia comunicativa 3, adeguatezza stilistica 1, correttezza morfosintattica 4, adeguatezza e ricchezza lessicale 3, ortografia e punteggiatura 1; for speaking, efficacia comunicativa 4, correttezza morfosintattica 4, adeguatezza e ricchezza lessicale 3, pronuncia e intonazione 1.

What it does not publish is a pass mark, or a minimum per section. An Italian government information sheet, meanwhile, states for both B1 exams that the maximum is {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points, the minimum is 55, and each skill carries a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_STANDARD_SECTION_MAX}} — which is the published rule for the **standard** CILS UNO–B1, on Siena's *Linee guida*. Siena's own general assessment page describes B1 out of 100 and never mentions the citizenship module.

One reading is that raw marks out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} are rescaled onto the 100-point reporting scale. No official document says so. We are reporting both figures against their sources and leaving them unresolved.

*Our own practice tool uses a floor of {{CILS_B1C_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} per section and a total of at least {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}}. Those numbers are ours, derived from the published standard-B1 rule; they are not an official threshold and Siena publishes none for this module.*

## Perugia: the CELI citizenship module

CVCL at Università per Stranieri di Perugia calendars its citizenship exam as **B1 CELI 2 i cittadinanza** — the suffix "i" marks the *immigrati* series. Its exam description gives a total duration of 2 hours and 30 minutes across four parts: Comprensione della Lettura; Produzione ed Interazione scritta; Comprensione dell'Ascolto; and the oral. The same document is our source for those four parts carrying equal 25% weight, but that detail reached us as a report of the document rather than a transcription of it, so we give it as CVCL's description and not as a quoted figure — confirm it on the exam-description PDF at <https://www.unistrapg.it/sites/default/files/docs/certificazioni/celi-i-b1-descrizione-esame.pdf>.

The writing paper is two texts, not one: a formal e-mail of minimum 40 and maximum 50 words, and an informal message of minimum 80 and maximum 90 words. The listening runs 20 minutes. The oral runs 10 to 12 minutes and covers self-presentation, image description, intercultural mediation, civic topics and dialogue.

Unlike Siena, Perugia publishes thresholds for this module. Its criteria document states that the written part is passed with a score of 72 or above, the oral with 11 raw (doubled to 22), and the exam overall with 92 or above out of a total of 160.

**Do not carry that 92 anywhere else.** The generic CELI 2 (B1), a different exam, publishes an overall pass band of 94 to 160. Both are B1, both total 160, both require 72 written and 22 oral, and their aggregate cut-offs differ by two points. We have no official statement explaining why.

## Two things the level does not tell you

**It does not tell you which module you need.** The government texts specify a level and a list of certifying bodies. No official source we could reach says that only a citizenship-branded module counts, and none says a generic B1 from the same body counts either. If you are considering a generic module, ask your Prefettura or Consolato and the awarding body before you enrol.

**It does not tell you what marking can see.** Siena's speaking criteria include *pronuncia e intonazione* — pronunciation and intonation — worth one point. Any tool that works from a transcript, ours included, cannot assess that. It is one point out of twelve, and it is one point we cannot estimate for you.

## FAQ

**Is "CILS B1 Cittadinanza" a legal category?**
No official source we could reach names a citizenship-specific exam variant in law. The law speaks of certification at not below B1 from a recognised body.

**Can I bank a passed section and re-sit the rest?**
On the CILS citizenship module, no — Siena says so explicitly. Perugia's regulation provides a capitalizzazione mechanism for the written part versus the oral, for one year at 70% of the fee, but we found no official confirmation that it operates for the cittadinanza module. Ask CVCL.

**What score do I need on the CILS citizenship module?**
Siena does not publish one. Any specific figure you see elsewhere, including ours, is somebody's derivation.
