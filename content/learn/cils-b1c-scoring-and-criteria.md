Two official sources describe the marks for this exam, and they do not describe the same scale. We are going to set both out and leave them unresolved, because that is the honest state of the published record.

**Siena's own criteria document for B1 Cittadinanza adds up to {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} points** — four abilità (skills) at 12 points each. **An Italian government information sheet on the CILS B1 exams says the maximum total is 100 with a minimum of 55**, each skill on 20 with a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}}. No document we could reach explains how one becomes the other, and Siena's general page on assessment discusses B1 out of 100 without ever mentioning the Cittadinanza module.

## The other figure, and who publishes it

The information sheet published by the Istituto Italiano di Cultura in Algiers — an Italian government source — states, for both B1 exams it covers, that "il punteggio massimo complessivo è di 100 punti. Il punteggio minimo è 55" (the maximum overall score is {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points; the minimum score is 55), with each skill on 20 and a minimum of {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} points per skill.

That 100/55/11 rule is independently confirmed for the **standard CILS UNO-B1** exam by Siena's own Linee guida, where five skills at 20 points each make 100. The unresolved question is whether it also governs the Cittadinanza module, whose own criteria document counts to 48 across four skills.

We are not going to tell you that the 48 is rescaled to 100, or that the government sheet simply reuses the standard exam's numbers. Both would be guesses, and this is not a topic to guess about.

## No published pass mark

Here is the fact that matters most and disappoints most: **Siena does not publish a pass mark, or a per-section minimum, for the CILS B1 Cittadinanza module.** The criteria document, the syllabus, the sample paper, the assessment page and the FAQ are all silent on it. Any specific claim of the form "you need this many points out of {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}}", or "6 out of {{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} per section", that you find online almost certainly originates with a school or a video rather than with the university.

Our practice product does show numbers: a **{{CILS_B1C_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_SECTION_MAX}} floor per skill** and **{{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_FLOOR}}/{{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}} overall**. Those are **ours**, and here is exactly how we got them — from the published standard CILS UNO-B1 rule, {{CILS_STANDARD_FLOOR}} out of {{CILS_STANDARD_SECTION_MAX}} per skill is 55 per cent, 55 per cent of 12 is 6.6, rounded up to 7, and four skills at 7 make 28. They are a preparation target we can defend as reasonable, not an official threshold, and we would rather show our working than let you mistake them for Siena's.

## Who marks it

Marking does not happen at your exam centre. Siena states that "Tutte le prove di esame sono valutate centralmente presso il Centro CILS, che ha elaborato i criteri per la valutazione" (all exam papers are assessed centrally at the CILS Centre, which developed the assessment criteria). The person who runs your speaking test is not the person who scores it.

## Questions readers ask

**So what score do I actually need?**
Nobody outside Siena can tell you, because Siena has not published it. Prepare to be comfortably adequate in all four skills rather than aiming at a number that has no official source.

**Which total will appear on my certificate?**
We could not verify what the certificate reports for this module. Check with your exam centre or the Centro CILS.

**Can I have my paper re-marked if I disagree?**
No official re-mark or appeal procedure is published. What is published is a right to view your papers within 30 days of results, under Law 241/90, by writing to the Centro CILS.

**Does the {{CILS_B1C_TOTAL_MAX}}-point criteria document apply to the standard B1 exam too?**
No. It is the B1 Cittadinanza criteria document. The standard CILS UNO-B1 has five skills and its own published rule of {{CILS_STANDARD_TOTAL_MAX}} points, 55 to pass.
